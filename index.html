Bipartisan Senate pushback on Trump's Russian diesel purchase

Facts

  • Trump announced Oct. 9, after a call with Putin, a sanctions waiver to buy several hundred thousand tons of Russian diesel, with larger volumes promised in later months.
  • Six senators (Republicans Collins, Murkowski, Tillis, Curtis; Democrats Shaheen, Blumenthal) said the deal helps Putin and that Congress was not consulted; a later letter drew twelve signers, six from each party.
  • Critics say it conflicts with the 2022 ban on Russian energy imports and a sanctions law passed weeks ago. Rep. Fitzpatrick (R-Pa.) plans a bill and discharge petition to block it.
  • US diesel is at record highs; the administration says the purchase will lower fuel costs.

Left view

Democrats quoted by Roll Call and AP/PBS call it a gift to Putin that undercuts Ukraine and the administration's own sanctions law. Zelenskyy said it “gives Putin money.” (Left-leaning opinion outlets were not retrieved today.)

Right view

Barrasso (R-Wyo.) on CBS argued Trump is prioritizing energy costs and sees the deal as leverage over Putin; Emmer defended it as well. Trump has blamed Ukrainian strikes on Russian energy sites for part of the price spike. Several Republicans, notably Tillis, broke with him. (Right-leaning outlets were not retrieved today.)

Watch for

Energy experts quoted by Roll Call say Russian diesel would barely move US supply but could send billions to Moscow. Watch for a House discharge petition, any legal challenge, and whether Congress moves to block the waiver.

Fallout from federal search of Rep. Ilhan Omar's home

Facts

  • Homeland Security Investigations agents executed a DOJ-obtained warrant at Omar's Washington home Monday, part of what DOJ calls an ongoing criminal investigation. No charges were named.
  • U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro told Fox News there are several investigations, including over financial disclosures.
  • Trump said he had nothing to do with it; Vance said he wasn't notified in advance and cited “serious allegations.”
  • Experts cite a DOJ manual and an informal 60-day norm against actions that could affect elections. A source says DOJ consulted its Public Integrity Section.

Left view

Omar's office called it an abuse of power; Raskin said the timing was “no accident” and amounted to intimidation. Legal experts quoted by Roll Call question the timing.

Right view

Pirro, speaking on Fox News, and Vance stress the seriousness of the allegations and say the administration will follow the facts. No Republican members of Congress were quoted in the Roll Call piece.

Watch for

Whether charges are filed and whether the case becomes a campaign issue in the final weeks before the midterms.

Hegseth's plan to livestream a military firing-squad execution

Facts

  • Trump approved a firing-squad execution of Nidal Hasan (2009 Fort Hood shooting) for Dec. 3; the Pentagon says it will be livestreamed. Appeals are still possible.
  • It would be the military's first execution in 65 years. Army regulation generally bars public executions but can be waived.
  • Criticism has come from both parties.

Left view

Adam Smith called it disturbing, Gallego a “snuff film,” Murray grotesque, and Reed said it hands a terrorist a global platform.

Right view

Rep. Andy Ogles backs it. Others on the right object: Tillis called it “beyond disgusting,” Rep. Austin Scott said it resembles what jihadists do, and Vance doubts it will happen.

Watch for

Possible court challenges, a waiver decision from the Army, and whether the Pentagon walks back the livestream.
