FAA orders inspections of hundreds of Boeing 737 Max jets over cracks

Facts

  • The FAA issued a directive Thursday requiring fuselage inspections of certain 737 Max 8, Max 9 and 737-8200 models after cracks were found in structural reinforcement on some older-generation 737s.
  • The cracks appeared in the "bear strap" reinforcing the area around the forward galley door cutout on the right side; the order covers 471 US jets and 1,429 worldwide.
  • No cracks have yet been found in Max jets specifically; the directive sets a mandatory inspection timeline and takes effect Sept. 10.

Right view

Watch for

Airlines face potential scheduling disruptions once inspections ramp up before the Sept. 10 effective date. Watch Boeing's stock and whether inspections turn up actual cracks on in-service Max aircraft.

FDA approves first mRNA flu shot, from Moderna

Facts

  • The FDA approved Moderna's mFlusiva (formerly mRNA-1010), the first seasonal flu vaccine using mRNA technology, on Aug. 5.
  • It is cleared for adults 50 and older; approval for the 65-plus group is "accelerated" and contingent on a follow-up postmarketing trial.
  • In late-stage testing it was about 27% more effective than a standard flu shot, with side effects (fatigue, joint and muscle aches) consistent with other mRNA vaccines.
  • The FDA had initially declined to review the shot before reversing course; Moderna expects it available for the 2026–27 flu season.

Left view

STAT and NPR highlight the efficacy gain and the FDA's reversal after earlier vaccine skepticism, framing approval as a win for mRNA science and pandemic-era research despite political headwinds.

Right view

Coverage from outlets tracking the administration's vaccine posture (U.S. News, Quartz) notes the conditional, age-limited approval and the required additional trial as signs of a more cautious, restrained FDA than during the COVID years.

Watch for

Approval reopens the door for Moderna's combo flu/COVID shot and pandemic-vaccine resubmissions. Watch uptake among older adults and whether insurers and CDC advisory panels recommend it broadly.
