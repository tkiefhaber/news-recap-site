Trump halts planned Iran strikes, says a deal is close

Facts

  • Trump said Sunday he is calling off a planned new wave of attacks on Iran, contingent on both sides "rapidly" reaching a deal, and claimed the "perimeters" of an agreement — including reopening the Strait of Hormuz — are in place.
  • The US–Israel war on Iran began Feb. 28, 2026; it has sharply curtailed Hormuz traffic, a chokepoint that once carried roughly a fifth of global crude and gas.
  • Late last week Iran's Revolutionary Guards reportedly turned back or stopped tankers in the strait, and the State Department warned US citizens across the Middle East.
  • Qatar, Oman and Pakistan are mediating an arrangement to let commercial traffic resume through Hormuz.

But coverage notes a real GOP split — the war has become a "sore spot" heading into the midterms, with some establishment figures sidelined (Fox News, AOL).

Watch for

Whether Hormuz actually reopens is the swing factor for energy: Brent near $90 could ease if the deal holds, or spike again if strikes resume. Watch mediators in Doha and Muscat, and any published deal text — past "deals" in this war have unraveled within days.

Trump announces Hamas disarmament deal — with big caveats

Facts

  • On July 31 Trump announced that Hamas agreed to disarm as part of a US-brokered plan, after the US-led "Board of Peace" and mediators from Egypt, Qatar and Turkey finalized a revised roadmap.
  • Hamas said it will begin disarming only if Israel ends strikes and withdraws from Gaza, and that surrendering heavy weapons is tied to creating a Palestinian state — which Israel's government rejects.
  • Trump says the plan paves the way for a new Palestinian government, a new Gaza police force and an International Stabilization Force.

Left view

NPR and mainstream outlets emphasize the caveats: the sequencing Hamas demands is exactly what Israel refuses, leaving the deal fraught with uncertainty. Coverage also notes Iran called Trump's floated Palestinian-relocation idea a continuation of plans to "wipe out" Palestinians (NPR, Deccan Herald).

Right view

Fox News billed it a "historic" milestone and quoted an Israeli official calling it the "first genuine opportunity" for a permanent settlement, while flagging deep skepticism that Hamas will actually follow through (Fox News, Newsweek).

Watch for

Implementation, not the announcement, is the test — specifically the order of Israeli withdrawal vs. Hamas disarmament and the unresolved statehood dispute. A durable deal would help calm regional oil risk; a collapse feeds back into the Iran escalation.

Right view

Watch for
