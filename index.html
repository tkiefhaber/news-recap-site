The Big Stories Today

Trump strikes Russian diesel deal with Putin; Zelensky calls it a “weak decision”

Facts

  • After a call with Putin, Trump said Russia would supply over 300,000 tons of diesel immediately, 500,000 tons in November and more later. The White House hasn’t said who pays or whether later shipments arrive before the Nov. 3 election (NPR/AP).
  • Treasury issued a temporary license for Russian diesel loaded as of Friday; sanctions wouldn’t apply to those deliveries until April 2027 (NPR/AP).
  • Russia has banned diesel exports since July after Ukrainian drone strikes on its refineries; U.S. diesel averages about $6.23/gal per AAA, down from a record $6.52 on Sept. 22.
  • On Saturday Trump criticized Zelensky over the reaction and said Ukraine should get “a new leader who can make a deal,” also calling for Ukrainian elections, which Ukrainian law bars during wartime (CNN).

Left view

NPR quotes Rep. Don Beyer (D-VA) calling the move “infuriating” and saying Trump isn’t trustworthy on Russia; CNN’s analysts say it badly damages the broader sanctions regime. Zelensky’s line that it was “a weak decision by strong partners” is the dominant framing in mainstream coverage.

Right view

I couldn’t retrieve right-leaning commentary on this story this morning (Fox, National Review and Washington Examiner pages didn’t surface it), so treat this as a gap. The administration’s own framing, per Trump’s post, is lower prices for farmers, ranchers and truckers as the top priority.

Watch for

Dan Pickering (Pickering Energy Partners) called it “helpful but not a needle mover,” estimating a 5–6% diesel supply increase by year-end. Gregory Brew (Eurasia Group) called it “a Band-Aid” and suspects Putin expects Kyiv to halt refinery strikes in return. Diesel futures fell about 4% Friday. The volumes equal roughly a day and a half of U.S. demand (EIA, via CNN). Also watch whether it stalls sanctions on top Russian-oil importers, which a law Trump signed last month calls for.

Pentagon to livestream Dec. 3 firing-squad execution of Fort Hood shooter

Facts

  • The Army set Nidal Malik Hasan’s execution for Dec. 3 at Fort Hood; on Oct. 8 the Pentagon said it will be livestreamed. Hasan was convicted of killing 13 people in 2009.
  • It would be the first U.S. military execution since 1961, which was by hanging. Hegseth first called it “public,” and said he didn’t care whether Hasan made final requests.
  • Hasan’s former attorney says Hasan has had no access to counsel; the Army disputes that. A civilian-court habeas petition could delay the date.

Left view

Critics call the livestream a spectacle that undercuts dignity and due process; the Trump administration criticizes public executions in Iran even as it plans this one. Sen. Chris Murphy (D-CT) is among those objecting.

Right view

Pushback isn’t only from the left: Fox affiliates’ headline says Hegseth faces “bipartisan backlash,” quoting GOP Sen. Thom Tillis calling the plan “beyond disgusting.” Supporters frame it as justice long delayed for victims.

Watch for

Legal challenges and any Pentagon change to the livestream plan before Dec. 3; whether Congress weighs in.

ICE agent shoots man in the Bronx; Mamdani demands ICE leave New York

Facts

  • On Oct. 8 around 4 p.m., an ICE agent shot Oscar Belgal, 28, in the neck in Marble Hill, firing seven rounds, per NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch. His 5-year-old son was in the car and unhurt; Belgal is in stable condition.
  • DHS says agents were arresting him and links him to the Trinitarios gang. Court records show a 2023 grand larceny plea and a pending 2025 drug-sale charge.
  • NYPD video shows a plainclothes agent approach the car with a drawn gun; the shooting itself wasn’t captured. Protests followed in the Bronx.

Left view

Mamdani called ICE “rotten to its core” and said it “must be abolished,” questioning why seven rounds were fired into a car with a child inside.

Right view

DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin said Trump offered to hand Belgal to the NYPD and that Mamdani refused to have him detained; he said ICE enforces laws the mayor won’t. Fox News coverage led with Mamdani demanding the release of an “illegal immigrant.”

Watch for

Any federal or state review of the shooting, further protests, and whether the administration escalates enforcement in New York City.
