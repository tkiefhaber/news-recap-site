Iran hits Bahrain again as a third U.S. carrier arrives in the Gulf

Facts

  • On Sunday the IRGC said it fired six drones at a U.S. early-warning radar at Riffa air base in Bahrain and claimed a "successful impact." Bahrain says its air defenses stopped the attack. It was the second Iranian strike on Bahrain in three days, after roughly two months without one. Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the GCC condemned the attack (The National, Al Jazeera).
  • The USS Theodore Roosevelt entered CENTCOM's area on Sunday, joining the George H.W. Bush and George Washington, so the U.S. now has three carriers in the region. Bloomberg reports Trump doesn't see an immediate return to strikes but is keeping the military option open. The U.S. has also pulled non-essential staff from several regional posts.
  • Iranian state TV says six vessels have caught fire in Hormuz since Sunday, and the IRGC calls the U.S.-backed southern route a "minefield of fire." None of that is independently verified. UKMTO separately confirmed a ship was hit by a projectile Monday, with a fire but no casualties.
  • Speaker Ghalibaf says Iran won't accept "neither war nor peace." Tehran's seven conditions, sent through Qatar, include lifting the blockade and sanctions, and its foreign ministry rules out limits on enrichment. Pakistan is hosting FM Araghchi as a mediator.

Left view

NPR's recent coverage centers on Gulf states trying to head off a renewed U.S.–Iran war through Qatar-led talks. The worry is that the U.S. is escalating while allies who host American bases take the hits. Al Jazeera quotes analyst Saeid Golkar: neither side wants all-out war, but "the danger lies in miscalculation." Left-of-center opinion writing was thin overnight, and most outlets ran wire copy.

Right view

Hawkish coverage focuses on whether the U.S. is losing its grip on the Gulf. A Washington Examiner op-ed by John Hannah and Rena Gabber, published last month after Iranian missiles damaged the Fifth Fleet's Bahrain headquarters, argued that Iran now has real say over whether the Navy can return there. The renewed drone strikes put that argument back in play. As with the left, there was little fresh right-leaning commentary overnight; regional outlets and wires are leading.

Watch for

Oil is the tell. Brent steadied near $95–96 Monday after a two-week slide because the IEA now projects a fourth-quarter surplus of about 1.7M barrels a day. Hormuz traffic is close to pre-war levels, which cushions prices unless ships keep getting hit. A confirmed strike that hurts U.S. personnel in Bahrain would sharply raise the odds of renewed U.S. strikes, and that would fall in the middle of Trump's Asia trip (below).

The things to watch are any statement on Taiwan independence, a truce extension, rare-earth compliance, and any move toward a new Kim Jong Un meeting, which CSIS's Victor Cha sees as central to Trump's legacy push.
