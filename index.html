NATO's North Atlantic Council meets today on the Russian missiles that fell in Poland

Facts

  • Two Russian missiles and a jet-powered drone came down in southeastern Poland on Nov. 20, during Russia's overnight barrage on Ukraine. They landed near Nowy Lipowiec, Rudnik nad Sanem and Molodycz. Poland's defense minister identified one as a Kh-101 cruise missile. No one was hurt (Al Jazeera).
  • Hours earlier, Trump told Fox News that Putin had landed drones and "a couple of little missiles" in Poland "by mistake." PM Donald Tusk called the strike an intentional provocation timed to coincide with nearby Coalition of the Willing exercises (Kyiv Post, Washington Post).
  • Poland has requested F-35s and AWACS support under NATO's Eastern Sentry mission. It has not formally invoked Article 4 (Latin Times corrected an earlier headline that said it had). NATO's Mark Rutte called the strike a grave violation, and the North Atlantic Council meets Monday (10 Things/Reuters).
  • DNI Jay Clayton said over the weekend that Putin is "not America's friend in Ukraine," after Trump called him a friend at last week's Shenzhen summit (10 Things).

Left view

European and Ukrainian outlets are leading here. The Kyiv Post framed Trump's comment as downplaying the incursion just before the missiles landed. Analysts quoted by Al Jazeera called the strike a test of how far Moscow can push, and said NATO isn't showing "teeth." Ireland's RTÉ contrasted the Shenzhen summit's silence on Ukraine with Europe's Berlin pledges.

Right view

Commentary from the right is thin. Breitbart carried the wire story, with Trump's "mistake" line alongside Tusk's disagreement. Fox's own interview was the source of the remark. Clayton's comment shows that hawks inside the administration aren't echoing Trump's tone.

Watch for

Whether NATO announces more air-defense assets for Poland. Article 5 is widely seen as off the table, since the strike caused no casualties (Al Jazeera). Also watch whether Congress presses the White House on Trump's "mistake" framing.

Bessent says Treasury "needs answers" from Nvidia on China chip sales; Huang will testify in December

Facts

  • On CBS's "Face the Nation," Bessent said Nvidia hasn't delivered the answers he asked for within 10 days. He raised the possibility of a "culture of noncompliance" with export controls on advanced AI chips to China (CBS via Yahoo, Reuters).
  • Jensen Huang resigned as CEO after reports of affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy. CFO Colette Kress is running the company. Huang has agreed to testify before Senate Banking and House panels in early December (CBS).
  • Trump has said he'd be fine with Huang returning as CEO, so the president and his Treasury secretary are striking different tones (Reuters).

Left view

Center-left outlets focus on the national-security failure: whether China got backdoor access to top-end US chips, and why the White House is cool toward a crackdown. Bessent himself called the affair angle "kind of a canard" and asked whether bad actors lower in the company were responsible (CBS).

Right view

China hawks on the right want tougher export enforcement, and Bessent's line is popular with them. Trump's call to bring back Huang reflects the pro-business, pro-AI-champion wing. Mainstream and right outlets are both covering this as a split inside the administration.

Watch for

Huang's testimony in early December, any Commerce Department penalties or new export rules, and whether Nvidia's board names a permanent CEO. Each is a swing factor for AI stocks.

COP31 collapses with no deal, the first failed UN climate summit since Copenhagen in 2009

Facts

  • Talks in Antalya, Turkey, ended Sunday about 44 hours past the scheduled close, making it the longest UN climate summit ever. The final draft from co-presidents Australia and Türkiye didn't mention fossil fuels and was rejected (ABC Australia).
  • The EU, UK, Norway, Panama and vulnerable island states objected. Saudi Arabia and Russia backed leaving fossil fuels out, and China supported the text. A $120B adaptation-finance figure was dropped (ABC News).
  • About 75 countries backed a separate "35-by-35" electrification pledge. COP32 is in Addis Ababa next November (ABC News).

Left view

NPR and CNN framed it as a collapse over fossil fuels. WRI's Ani Dasgupta called it a failure of political leadership, and Ireland's climate minister, speaking for the EU, called the draft unconscionable (ABC News).

Right view

Hungarian Conservative read the collapse as proof of the EU's waning clout: without Washington's weight, Brussels couldn't pressure Riyadh. The piece quoted Panama's minister calling COPs a laughing stock. US conservative outlets have largely ignored the summit.

Watch for

Coalitions of the willing on electrification replacing consensus deals. Expect little direct market impact, but fossil-fuel producers come out ahead politically.
