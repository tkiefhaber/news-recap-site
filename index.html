AI stocks slide as Nvidia weighs a $250B backstop for OpenAI

Facts

  • Nvidia is reportedly in talks to guarantee up to $250 billion in financing tied to OpenAI's data-center buildout — including a 10-gigawatt project in southern Ohio developed by SoftBank's SB Energy. Total spend could top $500 billion.
  • Nvidia fell about 5% to roughly $196 and slipped further overnight; AMD dropped ~8%. Chip weakness pulled the Nasdaq lower.
  • The sell-off deepened globally: South Korea's Kospi tumbled more than 10% as SK Hynix and Samsung sank double digits.
  • Bears including Jim Chanos and Michael Burry flagged the "circular" structure — Nvidia effectively financing buyers of its own chips.

Left view

Vox- and Atlantic-style commentary casts this as evidence the AI boom is propped up by financial engineering rather than real cash flow — a concentration risk that could hit ordinary 401(k)s if the trade unwinds. Concern about a few mega-caps carrying the whole market is front and center.

Right view

Market-oriented outlets (WSJ, CNBC, Reason-adjacent commentators) split: some see healthy price discovery and legitimate worry about vendor financing; others argue this is a normal capex supercycle and that cheap Chinese open-source models, not accounting, are the real threat to US chip demand. The framing leans "let markets sort it out."

Watch for

Whether other AI names disclose similar vendor-financing arrangements, and Nvidia's next earnings guidance. Strategists warn that with a handful of stocks driving index gains, a sustained AI repricing could drag the S&P and Nasdaq even as the broader economy holds.

Watch for

Pump prices track the Iran off-ramp almost directly — a credible ceasefire could ease oil and gas within weeks, while expanded strikes risk another leg up. Expect both parties to hammer gas prices through the fall; watch AAA's weekly average and crude's reaction to today's Trump-Netanyahu readout.
