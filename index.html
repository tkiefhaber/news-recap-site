The Big Stories Today

Trump takes the UN podium as Iran floats reopening Hormuz

Facts

  • Trump addresses the general debate of the 81st UN General Assembly today. White House previews point to the Gaza 20-point plan, Western Hemisphere policy, and keeping Iran from a nuclear weapon.
  • Japanese wire Kyodo reported Tuesday that Iran has offered, through intermediaries, to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if Washington eases military pressure and lifts its blockade of Iranian ports. A senior Iranian official said there is a possibility of moving toward an agreement but that Washington must show seriousness.
  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC that all Iranian airlines will be shut down starting Wednesday — the latest step in Operation Economic Outcast, the whole-of-government economic campaign launched Aug. 24.
  • Brent crude for November delivery slipped about 0.6% to roughly $99.70 on the Hormuz report, after topping $100 earlier this month. The IEA has called the Hormuz disruption the largest supply shock in the history of the oil market.
  • The war is roughly six months old. Trump's approval sits below 40%, weighed down in large part by its economic fallout.

Left view

NPR framed the trip as Trump heading to the UN "looking for wins after a rough week," and the Washington Post noted he arrives at a body whose members he has spent the year alienating — speaking Tuesday and hosting Xi Thursday largely without allies standing beside him. The through-line on the left is that six months of war has produced $100 oil, a closed strait and no visible endgame, and that the Hormuz offer is Tehran negotiating from a position the administration insists it does not have.

Right view

UN Ambassador Mike Waltz says the pressure campaign is working, pointing to the rial sinking past two million to the dollar and spiking Iranian inflation. The Washington Examiner's editorial page has been the loudest skeptic of Hormuz diplomacy, reporting that a top Iranian security official attached a new list of demands to any deal and arguing that a leaked Iran–Oman version would bar US and Israeli vessels from the strait — a non-starter. The right's position is broadly: keep squeezing, don't trade sanctions relief for a promise.

Watch for

Whether oil holds near $100 or breaks down on diplomacy headlines — analysts' spread on a US–Iran resolution runs from $85 to $140 a barrel. A genuine Hormuz reopening would take two to four weeks to reach US pumps, which matters with the midterms six weeks out. Bessent's airline shutdown lands Wednesday, one day before the Xi summit, and China is the largest remaining buyer of Iranian crude.

CNN, Politico and MS NOW sue over being barred from the White House

Facts

  • Trump declared Friday that the three outlets were persona non grata at the White House, saying the decision reflected cumulative coverage over several years rather than any single story.
  • On Saturday, MS NOW's Akayla Gardner, CNN's Betsy Klein and Politico's Cheyenne Haslett each tried to enter the grounds and were turned away by the Secret Service, which seized their press passes.
  • The three organizations sued Monday, seeking an injunction to halt the ban and restore credentials. The case went to US District Judge Timothy Kelly, whom Trump nominated during his first term.
  • Fox News, ABC News, CBS News and NBC News said in a joint statement they would not take CNN's place in the television pool.
  • White House Correspondents' Association president Jacqui Heinrich — a Fox News correspondent — said the revocations violate the First Amendment.

Left view

CNN and MS NOW call the ban a blatant First Amendment violation and a textbook case of viewpoint discrimination, given that Trump stated his reasons openly. Axios and NBC News both note the historical marker: presidents have feuded with outlets for a century, but none has unilaterally barred specific news organizations from covering the building.

Right view

This one does not split cleanly. Fox News signed the joint statement refusing to fill CNN's pool slot and its own correspondent, as WHCA president, called the move unconstitutional — Fox's news side quoted an attorney predicting the White House simply will not back down. Further right, the pushback is about hypocrisy and entitlement rather than the merits: PJ Media resurfaced CNN commentary that once entertained Biden banning Fox News, and the American Spectator argued the press confuses newsgathering rights with a constitutional entitlement to a hard pass.

Watch for

Kelly's ruling on the preliminary injunction is the near-term event. The governing precedent is the DC Circuit line from the 2018 Acosta litigation: the White House cannot pull a credential without due process where the space is generally open to press and no national-security rationale exists. A loss would likely be appealed fast; a win for the outlets would not stop the administration from narrowing pool access by other means.

Zelensky meets Trump in New York, days after the new Russia sanctions law

Facts

  • Zelensky and Trump agreed on a Sept. 20 call to meet today on the UNGA sidelines. Zelensky said the meeting "could change a lot" and arrived in New York with roughly 20 engagements scheduled.
  • It is their first meeting since Trump signed the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 on Friday. The law targets Russian energy and defense industries and the shadow fleet of tankers, and lets the president impose tariffs of up to 100% on the top five buyers of Russian oil and gas.
  • The House cleared the final version 262–159 on Sept. 16 after the Senate passed it 86–11 in August.
  • Zelensky says a new drone agreement is ready to sign. The trip follows Ukraine's largest drone attack on Moscow to date.
  • Kyiv Post noted the initially published UNGA schedule omitted the bilateral, which fed a day of uncertainty before Ukrainian officials confirmed it.

Left view

The bill split Democrats — 58 voted yes, 152 no. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries explained his no vote by arguing the law hands Trump extraordinary discretion: he can waive the sanctions it contains while gaining broad new tariff authority. Ukraine advocacy groups on the same side of the aisle disagree; Razom for Ukraine called passage momentous. NPR and CBS both framed the floor fight as leadership opposition being overcome rather than a party-line win.

Right view

Mainstream Republican support was overwhelming, and the law carries the name of the senator who spent years pushing it. The dissent came from the non-interventionist right: Reps. Thomas Massie, Andy Harris, Ralph Norman, Keith Self, Tom McClintock, Chip Roy and Warren Davidson voted no, arguing secondary tariffs on Russia's buyers are a tax on Americans and that Congress keeps delegating trade power to the executive.

Watch for

The operative question is enforcement, not passage: the top five buyers of Russian crude include China and India, and hitting either with 100% tariffs collides with Thursday's Xi summit and with an already strained global energy market. Watch whether Treasury designates any buyer at all in the next 30 days, and whether the drone deal Zelensky is carrying is a purchase, a co-production arrangement, or a memorandum.

Washington denies Abbas a visa; the General Assembly votes 152–3 to let him speak by video

Facts

  • The Trump administration extended its visa ban on Palestinian officials ahead of high-level week, blocking President Mahmoud Abbas and his delegation from traveling to New York.
  • The General Assembly then adopted a resolution allowing the State of Palestine to participate virtually in the general debate of the 81st session, by a recorded vote of 152 in favor, 3 against and 4 abstentions.
  • Abbas is expected to address the chamber by video, pressing for wider recognition and renewed momentum behind a two-state solution.
  • The push follows last year's wave of recognitions by the United Kingdom, France, Canada and other Western governments.
  • France plans to convene a follow-up meeting on the two-state solution this week. The EU had urged Washington to lift the travel ban.

Left view

Al Jazeera and European coverage treat the visa denial as a misuse of the host-country agreement — the US committed under the 1947 UN Headquarters Agreement to admit delegates to the world body, and using that gatekeeping against the leader of an entity most UN members now recognize as a state is the point of contention, separate from anything Abbas would say. The 152–3 margin is read as the assembly routing around Washington.

Right view

Israel Hayom's framing is that Trump, Netanyahu and Iran are the center of gravity this week and the Palestinian item is a sideshow with no mechanism behind it. The broader right-leaning argument, consistent with Netanyahu's standing position, is that recognition granted in the aftermath of Oct. 7 rewards the attack, that Abbas presides over an authority with no elections since 2006 and no writ in Gaza, and that a vote to permit a video feed changes nothing on the ground.

Watch for

Whether any additional government announces recognition during high-level week, and what France's follow-up conference actually produces — last year's version generated declarations, not implementation. The harder metric is Gaza itself: the 20-point plan has stalled beyond the ceasefire, hostage exchange and aid surge, with reconstruction frozen, Hamas still armed and Israel holding more than half the strip.

US and Chinese negotiators open talks in New York ahead of Thursday's Trump–Xi summit

Facts

  • Bessent and Vice Premier He Lifeng began trade talks in New York this week to set up the leaders' meeting at the White House on Thursday, Sept. 24.
  • The agenda covers trade and investment, artificial intelligence, the Iran war, rare earth minerals and a possible extension of the existing trade truce, including tariff cuts.
  • The negotiation runs on different legal footing than a year ago: in February the Supreme Court ruled 6–3 in Learning Resources v. Trump that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act does not authorize the president to impose tariffs, invalidating both the fentanyl-specific and reciprocal tariff programs.
  • Bloomberg describes the truce as fragile, with both sides maneuvering for the upper hand rather than arriving with a deal in hand.

Left view

Center-left and wire coverage is largely descriptive, with the critical note being that a summit convened this quickly, without a negotiated text, tends to produce a communiqué rather than enforceable terms — and that the tariff ruling left the administration reaching for other statutory authorities, which is a slower and narrower toolkit than the one it lost.

Right view

The Wall Street Journal editorial line has consistently held that the IEEPA tariffs were legally unsound regardless of their merits as policy, and that a durable China arrangement has to run through Congress. Hawks on the right are focused on rare earths and AI chips rather than the headline tariff number, arguing any extension of the truce that leaves Beijing's export-licensing leverage intact is a bad trade.

Watch for

Three concrete outputs: whether Beijing loosens rare earth export licensing, whether the truce is extended and for how long, and any movement on advanced-chip export rules. The Iran file is the wild card — China is the principal remaining buyer of Iranian crude, so a Hormuz arrangement and the Thursday summit are more connected than the separate agendas suggest.

Gas at $4.48 and the affordability fight six weeks from the midterms

Facts

  • AAA's national average for regular sits at about $4.475 a gallon today, up from $4.44 on Sept. 17 and $4.15 on Sept. 4. AAA reported a 13-cent jump in a single week.
  • Gas is more expensive in every state than it was a year ago. Prices peaked in May, fell through June and have climbed again all month.
  • The Fed raised its target range a quarter point to 3.75%–4% on Sept. 16 — its first hike in more than three years — on a 12–0 vote. Chair Kevin Warsh said inflation has been too high for too long; 16 of 19 FOMC members expect at least one more increase this year.
  • Fortune reported Trump characterizing higher pump prices as a very inexpensive price to pay for the Iran war.
  • Election Day is Nov. 3: all 435 House seats and 35 Senate seats. Primaries concluded Sept. 15. Two Trump-aligned super PACs have reserved more than $150 million in ads, much of it in previously safe red districts that have become competitive.

Left view

MS NOW's opinion page argues Senate Republicans committed political malpractice by adjourning before the midterms with nothing on affordability, leaving members to run on a war-driven price shock they chose not to legislate against. Polling cited in that coverage has 63% of voters saying the administration has made the economy worse, up from 52% a year ago — including about a quarter of Republicans.

Right view

Fox News has run field reporting from swing states on drivers squeezed at the pump, and Axios reported Republicans privately fretting about inflation's midterm drag. A conservative group aligned with GOP leadership circulated polling showing inflation as the top issue at 25%, more than double the second-place concern, with Trump's handling of inflation at 34% approval. The counterargument from the pro-war right is that the price is temporary and the strategic result is permanent — which is a harder sell at $4.48.

Watch for

The transmission chain is short: a Hormuz reopening lowers crude within days and pumps within two to four weeks, which would land right at the start of early voting in most states. If it does not happen, the risk runs the other way — the Fed is already leaning toward another hike, and a second oil leg up would force a choice between fighting inflation and cushioning a slowdown. Watch the next CPI print and the AAA weekly average as the two numbers most likely to move Senate race polling.
