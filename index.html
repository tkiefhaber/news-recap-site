Mississippi Senate race comes down to a sliver; Alaska still counting

Facts

  • In Time's latest update, Democrat Scott Colom led Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith by about 1,138 votes (49.0% to 48.9%) with about 96% counted. Earlier counts reported by the Mississippi Free Press had the gap at a few dozen votes. AP hasn't called it.
  • Affidavit (provisional) ballots get counted within five business days, and mail ballots postmarked by Election Day count if they arrive within five days. Colom's campaign estimates about 15,000 affidavit ballots are outstanding.
  • A Colom win would make him the first Democrat elected to the Senate from Mississippi since 1982. In Alaska, Dan Sullivan (R) vs. Mary Peltola (D) is still a toss-up with a slow count.

Left view

The Mississippi Free Press, a progressive-leaning nonprofit, and Time both present a Colom win as a historic breakthrough in the Deep South and a sign of how far the wave reached.

Right view

Hyde-Smith's campaign told WLBT it is confident she wins once every legal ballot is counted. Republicans are focused on ballot verification, and NBC News reports the race could become a fight over individual ballots. Conservative coverage so far is light.

Watch for

Experts quoted by Time don't expect a result until late this week or next. A margin this thin points to legal fights over affidavit ballots and possibly a recount. Whatever happens, these two races only change how big the majority is, not who controls the Senate.
