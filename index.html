The Big Stories Today

FAA grounds every U.S. departure for four hours; Duffy blames "incredibly old" system and asks for $50B

Facts

  • Shortly after 3:30 p.m. ET Friday the FAA reported unstable radar and communications at all Air Route Traffic Control Centers. It ordered a nationwide ground stop at 3:54 p.m. and lifted it at 8:15 p.m. About 3,500 planes were already in the air (ABC News).
  • FlightRadar24 counted more than 6,600 cancellations and 8,600 delays. It was only the second nationwide ground stop since 9/11; the last one was in 2023.
  • Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said switches and routers on an analog telecom backbone "got overloaded with data." He said the new SMART AI system played no role. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no sign of a cyberattack so far but didn't rule one out. The White House told CBS there was no indication of a "nefarious act."
  • At a White House briefing, Duffy asked Congress for $50 billion to overhaul air traffic control, up from the $30 billion Trump had requested (The Express via AOL).

Left view

Democrats are treating it as a failure of oversight. House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries called it an "unprecedented breakdown," Sen. Tammy Duckworth said the system is supposed to rely on redundancies that clearly failed, and Sen. Maria Cantwell said the Commerce Committee will investigate (ABC News). Left-leaning coverage (Irish Star, New Republic) seized on Mullin's halting explanation and Duffy's visible discomfort, casting it as an administration short on answers.

Right view

Right-leaning outlets mostly accept Duffy's diagnosis that this is decades of underinvestment finally coming due. They frame the $50B request as a test for the new Democratic Congress. RedState and Fox have long echoed Duffy's line that delays will continue until Congress pays for modernization, and he warned Friday that "you will have more of these" without an upgrade (ABC7).

Watch for

Ripple effects on weekend schedules as crews and aircraft end up out of position. Airline stocks fell late Friday. Expect a Senate Commerce hearing and a fight over ATC money in the lame-duck and new Congress. The Thanksgiving travel rush starts in under two weeks, so another outage would hit at the worst time.

Bessent publicly pins the Nvidia–China spy mess on Lutnick: "Commerce has the pen"

Facts

  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC Friday that Treasury, State, Defense and USTR had backed adding Singapore-based Megaspeed to the Entity List for months. He said Commerce, which controls the list, never acted (Washington Post, HuffPost).
  • Megaspeed, reportedly one of Nvidia's biggest customers, is run by Alice Huang. U.S. intelligence believes she is a Chinese spy, and former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has acknowledged an affair with her. Axios reported Bessent confronted Lutnick in a Situation Room meeting, with Rubio, Hegseth and Greer siding with Bessent. Trump reportedly approved action three times.
  • The NYT reported Commerce's Megaspeed probe stopped abruptly in late 2025 after Nvidia's general counsel contacted investigators and cited a senior Commerce official. The case moved to a Justice Department criminal inquiry. Attorney General Todd Blanche says no conclusions have been reached on whether Alice Huang is a spy.
  • Commerce calls its relationship with Treasury "great." Lutnick will testify before Senate Banking on Dec. 10, and Republican Sen. John Kennedy is demanding answers (Fox News, New Republic).

Left view

The New Republic and MSNBC's Maddow Blog say this is no longer something Lutnick can survive the way he survived the Epstein story. They point out Commerce hasn't added a single company to the Entity List since October 2025, and they say Trump knew about the intelligence for months yet still traveled with Huang to China. The Bulwark calls it "Chipghazi" and frames it as a cover-up.

Right view

The right is split. Washington Examiner columnists and RedState side with Bessent; the Examiner points to Lutnick's sons' reported $362 million Nvidia stake as a conflict. Fox News plays it as a cabinet "war" but notes most of the account rests on Axios's sourcing. Erick Erickson argues Lutnick is being set up as the fall guy while China plays a longer game. Hudson's Michael Sobolik, writing in The Dispatch, says the administration's Nvidia deals undercut its own export-control strategy.

Watch for

Whether Megaspeed lands on the Entity List before Trump meets Xi in Shenzhen next week, and whether Lutnick lasts until Dec. 10. Nvidia, now down about 12% this week and under $4T in market cap, reports earnings Tuesday. Analysts expect tighter China-diversion rules to weigh on guidance (HSBC cut its target to $170 from $365).

Iranian threats push Bessent and other top officials onto military bases as Trump settles into "no war, no talks"

Facts

  • Bessent confirmed he moved from his home to a military base on Secret Service advice after IRGC death threats tied to his sanctions campaign, "Operation Economic Outcast." Rubio, Hegseth, Stephen Miller and AG Blanche have reportedly done the same (USA Today).
  • The war is in its ninth month. Two U.S. soldiers killed in Jordan bring the U.S. military death toll to 17. The FBI added IRGC-linked Shahram Poursafi to its Most Wanted Terrorists list on Nov. 10.
  • A U.S. official told the Washington Post that Trump doesn't want to resume the war "for now." AP describes the approach as a blockade and sanctions with no new strikes and no deal. Qatar's effort to reopen the Strait of Hormuz is stalled while it waits for direction from Washington.

Left view

Left-leaning outlets say the war has now reached the cabinet's own homes, and that Trump's mixed signals ("militarily effectively over" on Monday, "locked and loaded" by Thursday) leave allies and markets guessing (AP via ABC). The New Republic says GOP 2028 hopefuls, including a reportedly wavering Vance, are tying the party's recovery to ending the war.

Right view

Breitbart and Townhall highlighted Bessent's profane defiance and his argument that the threats prove the pressure campaign works, with the regime acting like a "cornered animal." Rubio rejected the idea of a stalemate and called Iran's demands "absurd." From the right, the Free Press's Michael Brendan Dougherty argues the war, which Congress never authorized, is the main reason Republicans lost the midterms.

Watch for

Former NSC official Michael Singh expects a long stalemate under the naval blockade (AP). Next week's Trump–Xi–Putin meeting at APEC in Shenzhen is the next chance to move Hormuz. Oil keeps a risk premium as long as tankers stay blocked, and any attack on a U.S. official on American soil would raise the odds of renewed strikes sharply.

Democrat Kreiss-Tomkins wins Alaska governorship outright, capping a big week of statehouse gains

Facts

  • Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins, 37, won 50.8% of first-choice votes, enough to avoid a ranked-choice tabulation. Trump-endorsed Republican Bernadette Wilson got 28.1%, Dave Bronson 13.6% and Treg Taylor 7.7% (Newsweek). He'll be Alaska's youngest sitting governor when he's sworn in Dec. 7, replacing a Republican (AP/NBC).
  • Together with Kansas, it's the second governor's office Democrats have flipped this week. The New Republic reports more than 75% of Americans will now live in states with Democratic governors.
  • Senate Democrats face a December leadership vote: Chris Van Hollen is challenging Chuck Schumer, who says he has the votes (Politico).

Left view

The New Republic calls it a "massive win" and part of a broader realignment, citing 22 Democratic trifectas. CNN frames Alaska as the latest result in the GOP's midterm "shellacking."

Right view

Conservative outlets are mostly writing post-mortems instead of defending the results. The Federalist ran "the nationwide collapse of the Republican Party is Trump's fault," and the Free Press blames the Iran war and fuel prices. In Alaska, RedState had warned in August that the party needed to unite behind one candidate. Three Republicans split nearly half the vote.

Watch for

Whether the Democratic statehouse wave turns into fights over redistricting and energy permitting, which matter a lot for Alaska's oil and gas leasing. Also watch how the Schumer–Van Hollen race shapes the new Senate majority's approach to Trump.

Tanker loses steering and hits a docked river cruise ship in New Orleans; 32 hospitalized

Facts

  • Around 12:30 p.m. local time Friday, an Atlantic Asphalt tanker lost steering on the Mississippi and struck the docked American Cruise Lines vessel American Heritage, which Fox reports was carrying 129 passengers. The Washington Post reports the riverboat sank and some passengers went into the river.
  • Mayor Helena Moreno said everyone is accounted for. Thirty-two people were taken to hospitals with non-critical injuries, and no one died. The NTSB has opened an investigation (case DCA27MM003).

Left view

There's little partisan framing on this one. Mainstream coverage (CNN, NBC, WaPo) has focused on the rescue and on the tanker "losing control," as the mayor put it.

Right view

Fox News' coverage is similar to the mainstream's and centers on the rescue and the fact that everyone is accounted for. No significant ideological split.

Watch for

The NTSB's preliminary findings on the steering failure, any fuel or asphalt spill on a major shipping lane, and the liability fight between the tanker operator and the cruise line. Port traffic near the French Quarter could stay restricted for a while.
