World capitals push back on the new Section 301 tariffs as Democrats vow to unwind them in January

Facts

  • USTR's proposal covers 14 economies, including China, the EU, India, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Vietnam and Switzerland, after an eight-month "overcapacity" probe. Mexico and Norway were spared for now (Reuters).
  • China faces a proposed 20% on top of existing duties, potentially more if the trade truce lapses Jan 10 (NY Post). Rates for deal partners are capped at their existing deal levels, such as 15% for the EU and Japan.
  • Reactions: Beijing called overcapacity a "false premise" and reserved countermeasures; Japan called the move regrettable; Singapore said it was very disappointed; Thailand said its cap was better than expected.
  • Public comments close Jan 5, 2027, with hearings from Jan 25. A post-midterm Reuters/Ipsos poll found 62% support repealing the tariffs.

Left view

MS NOW and Democratic leaders frame it as an end-run around the Supreme Court's February ruling against the IEEPA tariffs, after voters rebuked them at the midterms. Sen. Ron Wyden, the likely incoming Finance chair, called it another attempt to rebuild illegal tariffs.

Right view

The NY Post led with USTR Jamieson Greer's argument that rivals' industrial policies hollow out US manufacturing, while noting inflation concerns. Townhall's economy coverage says the duties are likely to add to inflation. Reason has long rejected the overcapacity rationale.

Watch for

The administration hopes the threat pushes India and Vietnam into deals, possibly around the Miami G20. Democrats plan legal and legislative challenges, but the Washington Examiner notes overriding a veto would need Republican votes. Economists see added pressure on goods prices just as the Fed weighs hikes.
