Anthropic closes up 17% in its record debut, but the S&P 500 falls 2% for a sixth straight loss as AI stocks slide

Facts

  • Anthropic (ticker ANT) priced at $83, above its $70–78 range, and raised about $81.3B, the largest IPO ever. It opened at $111.50 just before noon, traded between about $92.72 and $115, and closed at $97.17, up 17% (Forbes, StockTitan).
  • Volume topped 600M shares by early afternoon, more than SpaceX's first day, per CNBC via Forbes. Google, Nvidia, Broadcom and Microsoft agreed to buy $26B of a separate share class at the IPO price (prospectus via StockTitan).
  • The rest of the market sold off anyway. The S&P 500 fell 2.0% to 7,175.83. That's its sixth straight loss, the longest since 2024, and it's now 8.2% below last month's record. More than 90% of S&P stocks fell (AP).
  • Anthropic's partners fell too: Alphabet −3.5%, Amazon −3.9%, Nvidia −3.3%, Microsoft −2.0%, CoreWeave −6.1%. The VIX fear gauge rose to about 28, near its highest since March (StockTitan, The Weekend Spread).

Its broader framing is that a ~$1.5T AI company now answers to public shareholders while its products are being drawn into warfare. Partisan coverage of the debut itself was thin on both sides; business outlets led.

Right view

Right-leaning outlets have been less focused on the debut than on the broader AI-stock selloff. In earlier pieces, the Washington Examiner asked whether the IPO would "go supersonic." There was little new right-leaning commentary on the debut day. The bear case that skeptics cite is in the filing itself: Forbes points to an $80.7B first-half net loss, $117B in liabilities, and large lease commitments for compute.

Watch for

CNBC called the debut a key test of AI appetite. A weak follow-through would add to worries that the AI trade has peaked, with the 10-year yield above 5% and markets fully pricing a December rate hike.

The Trump–Xi–Putin summit ends with a short statement and no breakthroughs; Trump gives it a "12 out of 10"

Facts

  • The first three-way leaders' meeting, held in Shenzhen on the sidelines of APEC, lasted about 90 minutes (Slate). The joint statement covered UN reform, higher oil output, more talks on reducing nuclear risk (with no arsenal cuts), and sovereign immunity from international courts.
  • Trump hoped to discuss the Ukraine and Iran wars. Slate reports Xi and Putin didn't engage, and Putin said he is halting Ukraine talks after Ukraine's attack on the Crimea bridge. The declaration doesn't mention Iran (Times of Israel).
  • Trump told NBC the meeting rated a "12" and said Russia–Ukraine decisions won't be made without Ukraine at the table. He'll hold a White House news conference today at noon ET (ABC News).

Left view

Schumer called the trip a "historical embarrassment" and said Trump deferred to Xi and Putin and came back with nothing (ABC News). Slate's Fred Kaplan argues nothing important happened and that Putin gained standing just by being in the room. Democracy Now highlighted Trump calling Putin "my friend," and Poland's Donald Tusk replying, "God, protect us from such friends."

Right view

Not all conservatives are cheering. National Review called the trilateral "overhyped" and said it produced little on global security. Trump himself argues the meeting opened the door to nuclear-risk talks and more oil supply. Time's headline is closer to the middle: the summit made history but not much else.

Watch for

Look for details at today's presser, especially on the oil-supply pledge, which wire reports say faces implementation hurdles. Kaplan and European officials worry about a "new Yalta" and softer U.S. resolve on Taiwan. If Ukraine talks really are paused, expect more Russian pressure and pushback from European capitals next week.

Democrats nominate Jeffries for speaker 251-11 and split over whether to use the Dec. 11 funding deadline to end the war

Facts

  • House Democrats nominated Hakeem Jeffries for speaker 251-11 in a secret ballot. Katherine Clark (majority leader) and Pete Aguilar (whip) were unopposed. The floor vote is Jan. 3, and Jeffries would be the first Black speaker. Republicans chose Jim Jordan as House minority leader and kept John Thune as Senate GOP leader (NPR).
  • Rep. Sarah McBride was elected to the leadership team, the first trans member of congressional leadership (via Mueller, She Wrote).
  • Government funding runs out Dec. 11. Sens. Van Hollen, Sanders, Bennet and Duckworth say any stopgap must bar funding for the Iran war. Shaheen, Hickenlooper, Coons and Warner oppose a December shutdown. Schumer hasn't said what he'll do, and Jeffries has floated an extension into 2027 (Newsweek).
  • Republicans hold 53 Senate seats until January, so Democrats can block a bill. Thune called Van Hollen's demand unrealistic. A Reuters/Ipsos poll finds 71% want the new Congress to end the war (Newsweek).

Left view

Progressives see the deadline as their last leverage before Democrats take power. MS NOW and HuffPost covered the push to "play hardball," and Van Hollen says any shutdown would be Trump's choice. NPR focused on 11 new left-flank members, some backed by DSA, who withheld votes from Jeffries until he makes policy commitments.

Right view

Fox (LiveNOW) described a "restive party," with protesters heckling Jeffries in Denver and progressives criticizing his ties to the establishment. Republicans like Thune argue that using a shutdown as leverage over a war is short-sighted. They say Democrats would own the disruption weeks before they take the gavel.

Watch for

Senate Democratic leadership elections are in early December, and Van Hollen is challenging Schumer, so the shutdown fight is tied to a leadership race. Third Way warns Democrats lack the votes to win a standoff. The likeliest outcome is a short stopgap into January, though watch for war-funding amendments from Duckworth, Gillibrand and Warren.

Hegseth visits Caracas and signs a deal to restore U.S.–Venezuela military ties

  • Rodríguez has governed since U.S. forces captured Nicolás Maduro in January. AP notes her court-allowed term expired in July. Hegseth said Venezuela's oil makes the Strait of Hormuz "a nonfactor" for the Western Hemisphere (AP).
