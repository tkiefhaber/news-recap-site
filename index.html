Hot October inflation pushes 10-year yield past 5% and locks in a December Fed hike

Facts

  • October CPI rose 0.4% month over month and core rose 0.3%, both above forecasts. Core has now run at 0.3% or more in five of 2026's first ten months. There is no year-over-year figure because October 2025 prices weren't collected during the shutdown.
  • Energy cooled (+0.7% vs. +5.2% in September), but food rose 0.5%, transportation services 0.8% and shelter 0.3%.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield topped 5% (session high ~5.02%) and the 2-year jumped about 18 bp to 4.74%. CME FedWatch odds of a hike at the Dec. 8–9 meeting hit 100%.
  • Gov. Christopher Waller said a half-point hike should be considered if core stayed near 0.3% (it did); Dallas Fed's Lorie Logan wants at least two more quarter-point hikes. All 101 economists in a Reuters poll expect a December hike to 4.00%–4.25%.

Left view

Common Dreams framed the report as the Iran war "hammering consumers," citing Navy Federal economist Heather Long's estimate of roughly 4% annual inflation and a "big jump in grocery prices," and economists arguing real wages have stalled since the war began in February. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer blamed tariffs and the war.

Right view

The Daily Wire cast it as the opening of an economic showdown between Trump and the incoming Democratic majority. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blamed an energy supply shock from the Iran war and Strait of Hormuz disruption, while pointing to the 21.5% cumulative price rise under Biden; NEC Director Kevin Hassett called it "a disappointing report."

Watch for

October PPI on Nov. 13 and PCE on Nov. 25 will decide whether the Fed goes 25 or 50 bp. Markets priced about a 13% chance of a half-point move; some strategists warn a big hike would fight a war-driven supply shock with demand tools. Mortgage rates track the 10-year, so 5%+ yields mean pricier home loans heading into winter. Bond markets are closed today for Veterans Day; stocks trade normally.

Trump reverses course and questions whether the midterms were "honest"

Facts

  • In a Monday Truth Social post, Trump asked whether anybody "really" believed the election was honest, questioned how the polls could be "so wrong," and said "It's all about the machines." He did not name specific races.
  • Days earlier he had congratulated Democrats, said he'd work with them, and told the WSJ he'd "get along very well" with them. Democrats take control of both the House and Senate on Jan. 3.
  • A preliminary Pew Research review found pre-election polls got the direction right but understated Democrats' margin, especially in Georgia and Texas statewide races.

Left view

Schumer called the return to election denial "pathetic" and noted Trump faces large Democratic majorities for his final two years. The Washington Post framed it as Trump casting doubt on Democrats' "historic" wins; CNBC highlighted the revived voting-machine conspiracy.

Right view

Conservative pushback was notable: Megyn Kelly reposted the claim with "Just stop." In a Fox News roundup, conservatives largely blamed the losses on real-world conditions — Washington Examiner's Guy Benson called it "a catastrophic night," and Daily Wire's Matt Walsh and The Federalist's Sean Davis pointed to the Iran war, gas prices and grocery costs rather than fraud.

Watch for

Whether the claim turns into legal challenges or remains rhetoric — none had been filed in the coverage reviewed. Analysts expect it to sour any early cooperation on immigration and the economy with the new Democratic majorities.

Nvidia falls 5.5% on day two of the Jensen Huang spy-affair fallout

Facts

  • Nvidia's board has hired outside counsel for an independent probe of CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with a Chinese businesswoman alleged to have passed information to Beijing. Huang apologized for the affair; the board says it has "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were compromised.
  • Shares fell 5.47% Tuesday after a 4%+ drop Monday, making Nvidia the biggest drag on the indexes. Shareholders have sued, alleging the undisclosed relationship inflated the stock.
  • Trump called Huang "an honorable guy," said Huang told him he had no idea she was a spy, and confirmed the FBI is looking into it. China's foreign ministry called the allegations "smears."

Left view

Per CNBC, Schumer accused Trump of defending Huang at the expense of national security; Sen. Chris Coons (D) called for a probe of Nvidia (The Hill). Democrats tie the case to the administration's looser stance on chip sales to China.

Right view

Republican China hawks broke from Trump's tone: Sens. John Kennedy, John Cornyn, Josh Hawley and Pete Ricketts want FBI/intelligence-committee scrutiny of whether chips or secrets reached China (Washington Sun). Fox Business aired warnings about Chinese targeting of U.S. AI firms while relaying Trump's defense of Huang.

Watch for

Bloomberg says Nvidia's bull case now hinges on Huang surviving the probe. Export-control bills (AI Overwatch Act, Chip Security Act, MATCH Act) could ride on the lame-duck defense bill before Democrats take over in January — a direct risk to Nvidia's China revenue. Broader AI-stock sentiment is already fragile after last week's Nasdaq rout.

House shelves Hegseth impeachment, 211–204, as Democratic absences decide it

Facts

  • The House voted 211–204 to refer Rep. Thomas Massie's (R-Ky.) impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee, effectively killing them for this Congress. Massie was the lone Republican against the motion.
  • The articles accused Hegseth of launching the Iran war without congressional approval and of extrajudicial killings in the Caribbean boat strikes. Hegseth called the effort a "political spectacle."
  • Eighteen members missed the vote — 11 Democrats and 7 Republicans. Had the absent Democrats voted, the motion likely would have failed.

Left view

The Guardian, MS NOW and NBC emphasized Republicans blocking the effort; The Intercept said Hegseth "wove a web of lies" at his press conference; Mother Jones focused on the treatment of Black service members. Al Jazeera asked whether Democrats "saved" Hegseth, and Rep. Sydney Kamlager-Dove scolded absent colleagues.

Right view

Breitbart framed it as House Republicans rejecting a partisan effort; Trump said Democrats "miserably FAILED," and the Pentagon called it a "TOTAL VICTORY." Reason and CBS treated it as a procedural tabling.

Watch for

Hakeem Jeffries says he'll consult members on reviving impeachment after Democrats take the House Jan. 3 (Time notes Hegseth could become only the third cabinet secretary impeached). Removal would still need two-thirds of the Senate — about a dozen Republicans.

Russia, Ukraine and U.S. wrap two days of Abu Dhabi talks; envoys head to Moscow

Facts

  • The first U.S.-brokered trilateral talks since February ran Tuesday and Wednesday in Abu Dhabi, with envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. Kushner called day one "constructive"; no agreement was announced as the final session ended.
  • The focus shifted to a post-war security framework for Ukraine and Europe. Moscow still demands the rest of Donetsk; Kyiv wants to freeze the front line. Zelensky said the U.S. asked whether Kyiv would "trade the Donbas for security."
  • Lavrov rejected European ceasefire demands, and the Kremlin refuses a European seat at the table; U.S. officials told the FT they want Europe included. Witkoff and Kushner may next travel to Moscow and Kyiv.

Left view

The Washington Post argued the U.S. handed Russia "an early win" by tying the talks to a broader European security deal Moscow wants. The BBC and much European coverage stressed Europe's push not to be sidelined.

Right view

Right-leaning U.S. outlets have given the talks thin coverage, largely echoing the White House's "cautiously optimistic" line (via Axios) and Trump's stated "strong inclination" to end the war; Secretary of State Marco Rubio called the talks "very productive."

Watch for

Ukrainian drone strikes have cut Russian refining capacity by about 30% (Reuters), which matters for diesel and crude markets. Any credible ceasefire signal would pressure oil further on top of Iran-diplomacy hopes; a breakdown would do the opposite. A possible Trump–Xi–Putin summit is also in play (WSJ).
