Election Day: polls are open, 57 million have already voted, and House and Senate control is on the line

Facts

  • All 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats and 36 governorships are on the ballot, along with about 145 statewide ballot measures. More than 57 million people voted early, according to the University of Florida Voting Lab (CBS News). Republicans hold the Senate 53-47 and have a razor-thin House majority.
  • Final New York Times/Siena polls show Democrats ahead in Michigan (El-Sayed 53, Rogers 42), Texas (Talarico 50, Paxton 43) and Kansas (Hamilton 49, Marshall 44). Other final polls show Maine (Jackson 51, Collins 45), Ohio (Brown 49, Husted 44), Alaska (Peltola 48, Sullivan 46) and Iowa (Turek 48, Hinson 47) within single digits. Nebraska's Ricketts and independent Osborn are tied at 49 (Forbes roundup). Silver Bulletin's generic-ballot average is D+8.9.
  • CBS's Battleground Tracker projects 229 Democratic House seats, with a range of 215-237. Kalshi bettors give Democrats about a 94% chance of winning the House.
  • Trump closed his campaign Monday in Washington, saying Republicans had a "very strong shot." He also acknowledged that the sitting president usually doesn't win the midterms.

Left view

Mainstream and left-leaning coverage frames the vote as a verdict on Trump's second term. NPR and CBS emphasize cost-of-living pressure and an unpopular Iran war, and the Forbes/NYT poll roundup points to Democratic leads in red states like Texas and Kansas. Democrats on the ground in Michigan argue that Haley Stevens's defection is backfiring. CBS quotes Rep. Debbie Dingell saying the endorsement is "galvanizing" voters behind El-Sayed.

Right view

Fox News presents the night as a turnout test for a GOP playing defense. It quotes RNC chair Joe Gruters: "If our voters turn out, we win." It also notes that Fox's own Power Rankings moved Kansas to toss-up, and it flags the ballot-integrity measures (voter ID, noncitizen voting) on the ballot in 11 states. The Washington Examiner's Guy Benson has warned for weeks that the GOP faces a "reckoning" while noting Democrats remain unpopular themselves.

Watch for

Kentucky's 6th District, which Trump won by about 15 points, is an early bellwether (Fox). Alaska's ranked-choice count could take days or weeks, and Maine also uses ranked choice. If the Senate comes down to Alaska, expect a certification fight there. Markets have largely priced in a split Congress. A Democratic sweep would put tariffs, Iran war funding and oversight at the center of 2027.

US hits Iran's Qeshm Island on election eve in "defensive" strikes as a Doha draft deal hangs in the balance

Facts

  • US officials told the Wall Street Journal, CBS and others that American forces carried out "limited defensive strikes" Monday night on Qeshm Island near the Strait of Hormuz. Officials said the strikes answered Revolutionary Guard fire on a US aircraft. Qeshm reportedly hosts an underground missile facility and a naval base (RFE/RL). Iranian media reported explosions there and in Bandar Lengeh. No casualties were reported.
  • The WSJ says this is one of a string of previously undisclosed limited strikes, kept quiet to avoid signaling a return to full-scale war. The IRGC claims it captured two US reconnaissance drones and downed an MQ-9 (Al Jazeera).
  • Asked whether he'd strike Iran after the midterms, Trump said "No." He posted that a deal "could happen" and that Iran "wants a settlement." Per the WSJ, though, US officials told Gulf states to prepare for a short, intense campaign shortly after the election. AP reports Netanyahu is pushing for exactly that.
  • Iran's foreign ministry says "the ball is in the US court." Tehran says Hormuz can reopen within two days if the US lifts its naval blockade and ends hostilities. Separately, a vessel off Oman was hit by an "unidentified aerial object," killing one Indian crew member. UKMTO logged a record 30 attacks on shipping in October (CBS).

Left view

Common Dreams and MS NOW lead with the timing: strikes hours before the vote, after Trump said there would be none before the midterms. NIAC's Jamal Abdi calls it an unauthorized war waged in secret and urges Congress to cut funding. Sen. Chris Murphy, citing a classified briefing, called a reported post-election plan to resume major attacks "insane." NPR's Morning Edition reports Iran is preparing for renewed attacks after the midterms.

Right view

Fox News's live coverage frames the strikes as protecting US forces and shipping while talks continue. It pairs them with Trump's "give them the hammer" warning and his reported preference, per Axios, to let negotiations play out. The Jerusalem Post reports, citing officials, that the strikes followed Iran downing a US drone. It treats them as calibrated, not a policy shift.

Watch for

Oil gave back part of Monday's 7% plunge, with Brent near $106-107. Emirates NBD's Daniel Richards says the strikes "renewed uncertainty" around the diplomatic opening. Swissquote's Ipek Ozkardeskaya notes the election can shift expectations but can't reopen shipping lanes on its own. A signed deal that ends the blockade is the big downside risk for crude, and a post-election air campaign the big upside one. Gas prices follow with a lag of a week or two.

Supreme Court refuses DOJ bid to wall off 20,000 California mail ballots

Facts

  • On Monday the Supreme Court denied the administration's emergency request to make California set aside mail ballots from about 20,000 people DOJ says are likely noncitizens. CBS describes a one-line unsigned order with no noted dissents. Fox reports Justice Elena Kagan acted on it without referring it to the full court.
  • DOJ had lost at every level. US District Judge David Carter called the government's identification process "rife with uncertainty and errors," and a divided 9th Circuit panel declined to intervene. More than 6 million California mail ballots have been returned.
  • Fox News also reports that Chief Judge James Boasberg in Washington said it was "likely" the administration violated the Constitution by taking over election duties from states and Congress. He blocked further federal mail-ballot requests to California. (Other outlets reviewed hadn't yet reported this.)

Left view

CBS, OPB and KPBS (AP) emphasize state officials' warning that the federal list is error-prone and could disenfranchise eligible citizens. They present the ruling as courts shutting down federal interference in state-run elections on the eve of the vote.

Right view

Fox News centers DOJ's argument that once illegal ballots are counted, the harm "is impossible to remedy." It notes the administration sought only to preserve the ballots, not cancel them, and highlights Judge Patrick Bumatay's 9th Circuit dissent. It also points out that California's 52 House seats matter to the majority math.

Watch for

The order ends the pre-election fight but not the underlying lawsuit, so DOJ can still contest the ballots after the count. Combined with the Alaska certification case, it sets the template for post-election litigation if close races hinge on contested ballots.

Ballot measures: California's billionaire tax (Prop 40) slips in final polls

Facts

  • Prop 40 would levy a one-time 5% tax on the wealth of roughly 200-250 California billionaires, with most of the money going to offset Medi-Cal and other health cuts. It's backed by SEIU-United Healthcare Workers West.
  • Support has eroded. PPIC's late-October likely-voter poll has it at 46% yes and 52% no, down from 52% support a month earlier (CalMatters). UC Berkeley IGS has it at 42-50 (KQED).
  • Opponents have raised over $205 million, including at least $102 million from Sergey Brin. Billionaire-backed Props 41 and 42 would nullify the tax if they get more votes, and Prop 42 polls at 51%.
  • Elsewhere: Washington votes on a millionaires' tax, and voter-ID or noncitizen-voting measures are on the ballot in 11 states (Fox News).

Left view

Supporters quoted by CalMatters argue that a clear majority still backs a "modest, one-time tax" on the very richest to keep health care affordable. KQED's poll coverage attributes the slide to a lopsided money fight. Berkeley IGS's Mark DiCamillo says the no side only has to "raise doubts." NPR's Planet Money notes economists studying the tax reached sharply different revenue estimates.

Right view

National Review calls the measure "fiscal self-sabotage," arguing that billionaire flight could cost California billions in ongoing income-tax revenue, more than the one-time tax raises. Tax Notes warns that a "one-time" levy would set a lasting precedent.

Watch for

Political Wire and others see Prop 40 as a 2028 test of the Democratic left's wealth-tax pitch. If it passes but Prop 42 gets more votes, expect it to land in court. California's count runs for weeks, so a close result won't be known tonight.

Israel: Arab parties back Eisenkot ahead of Herzog's coalition talks Wednesday

Facts

  • The Joint List (11 seats) and Ra'am (8) say they'll recommend Gadi Eisenkot for prime minister. Added to his bloc's 48, that's 67 of 120 seats, against Netanyahu's 53 (Al Jazeera, Jerusalem Post).
  • Joint List leader Yousef Jabareen says his party won't join a government while the West Bank occupation continues but would vote with one. That opens the door to a minority government.
  • Results are certified Wednesday, when President Herzog opens consultations. The nominee gets 28 days, extendable by 14, and the Knesset is sworn in Nov. 10. Herzog: the results "are an established fact."

Left view

Al Jazeera notes Eisenkot, Bennett and Liberman have all ruled out Arab parties in government, while Labor's Yair Golan urges working with them. It frames Netanyahu's unproven fraud claims as a bid to delegitimize a Jewish-Arab majority.

Right view

Likud warns that Zionist parties relying on the Arab bloc would be "dangerous to Israel" and has suggested it borders on treason (via Al Jazeera). The Jerusalem Post lists outcomes from a unity government to a new election and reports Likud and Ra'am remain in contact. US right-leaning coverage is thin.

Watch for

The opposition's first move may be ousting Knesset Speaker Amir Ohana (Likud). An Eisenkot-led government would likely be more cautious about joining renewed US strikes on Iran. That ties the outcome directly to the Hormuz story above.
