Ukraine's record drone barrage hits the Crimean Bridge two nights running

Facts

  • Russia's Defense Ministry reported downing about 1,300–2,000 Ukrainian drones over 15–21 regions across Sunday and Monday nights, the largest Ukrainian drone attack of the war. Moscow's mayor said hundreds of drones were aimed at the capital. All four Moscow airports were put under flight restrictions (Irish Times/Reuters, ABC News). None of these figures can be independently verified.
  • The Kerch (Crimean) Bridge was hit Sunday night and again Monday night. The Kremlin first called the damage minor, but by Tuesday Crimea's Russian-installed head said traffic was suspended. Two people were killed in a drone strike near Kerch, he said. Ukrainian outlets report a road span was badly damaged (ABC News, Euromaidan Press).
  • Fires hit industrial sites in Rostov and Krasnodar. Zelenskyy claims more than half of Russia's refining capacity is disabled; industry sources cited by Reuters put it nearer 30% (Fox News).
  • Russia is striking Dnipro River bridges in Kyiv. Zelenskyy says Moscow is "trying to divide Ukraine along the Dnipro." The first U.S.-led talks in nine months, held in Abu Dhabi, ended without a breakthrough. Estonia will close its Russian border crossings Dec. 1 (Irish Times, POLITICO).

Russia is targeting power links between western and eastern Ukraine. Zelenskyy is also complaining that SpaceX's Starshield is too slow. Coverage notes concern that Thursday's Trump–Xi–Putin meeting excludes Ukraine.

Right view

Facts

Three U.S. carriers are now in the region, including the newly arrived Theodore Roosevelt, supporting the blockade of Iranian shipping. Trump is weighing whether to resume full strikes or tighten the blockade (AP).

PAHO set to formally strip the U.S. of measles-elimination status today

Facts

  • The Pan American Health Organization concluded that measles has spread continuously in the U.S. for more than a year, and its public announcement is planned for today. The U.S. had held elimination status since 2000. The decision traces to a Utah outbreak chain that passed one year in June (KFF Health News, WSJ).
  • The CDC counts 5,412 confirmed cases in 2026 through Nov. 12, more than double all of 2025. KFF counts more than 8,000 illnesses, 830+ hospitalizations and 13 deaths over two years. Kindergarten MMR coverage is 92%, below the roughly 95% herd-immunity threshold (Fox News, NBC).
  • Getting the status back requires 12 months without sustained spread once current outbreaks end. Outbreaks are still active in South Carolina, Utah and Pennsylvania.

Left view

KFF Health News and NPR center the federal response. Former CDC deputy director Anne Schuchat says leaders spreading "disinformation and flat-out falsehoods" has been "unforgivable," and an unnamed CDC official alleges a deliberate downplaying of vaccine-preventable disease. MS NOW frames it as a scandal Health Secretary Kennedy must answer for.

Right view

Fox News presents it as a gap in community immunity rather than a vaccine failure, quoting doctors who say the shot is about 97% effective with two doses, and Dr. Marc Siegel urging vaccine clinics in low-coverage areas. The Washington Examiner's coverage is procedural and assigns no blame. HHS says the gaps predate this administration, notes $100M in added CDC funding, and says Kennedy and the CDC director still recommend MMR.

Watch for

Experts like former CDC official Paul Rota say regaining the status isn't "on the horizon" given expanding outbreaks. Expect pressure on school vaccine exemptions, more early infant doses (from 6 months) in outbreak areas, and congressional Democrats using January oversight power to probe CDC reporting delays.
