Michigan shock: Democrat Haley Stevens endorses Republican Mike Rogers over El-Sayed

Facts

  • Rep. Haley Stevens, who narrowly lost the August Democratic Senate primary to Abdul El-Sayed, endorsed Republican Mike Rogers on Sunday. She said she remains a Democrat and will back Democrats in about 90% of her races. The seat is open because Democratic Sen. Gary Peters is retiring.
  • The break followed a fight over a Rebellion PAC ad linking US military aid to Israel with grocery prices. Stevens called the ad antisemitic. El-Sayed said his campaign didn't make it and that he disagrees with it. Rep. Debbie Dingell then asked Stevens not to attend a Sunday unity event, calling her presence "way too distracting."
  • Schumer called it a "despicable betrayal." Stevens's chief of staff of eight years and several staffers resigned. El-Sayed's campaign says it raised more than $1.2 million afterward. Trump praised Stevens, invited her to join the GOP, and called El-Sayed a "jihadist." El-Sayed would be the first Muslim US senator.
  • More than 1.8 million Michiganders had voted before the endorsement. Reuters reports polls show El-Sayed has largely consolidated Democratic support.

Left view

NPR and Al Jazeera focus on the timing: less than 48 hours out, and after Stevens said midweek she still wanted El-Sayed to win. Al Jazeera notes an AIPAC-affiliated super PAC spent tens of millions on Stevens in the primary. Progressive figures were harsher. Fox News collected posts from Mehdi Hasan, Hasan Piker and Cenk Uygur, with Hasan calling Stevens "evil."

Right view

The Washington Examiner frames the endorsement as a sign of a broader problem in the Democratic Party. It leads with Stevens's claim that El-Sayed has been "getting angrier and angrier" and that pro-Israel Democrats are being made to feel unwelcome. Fox News calls it a "bombshell," quotes the RNC on Rogers's cross-party appeal, and notes GOP Rep. Anna Paulina Luna offered Stevens her office's help, praising her for "putting country over party."

Watch for

Stevens beat El-Sayed in Wayne and Oakland counties, drawing Black and Jewish Democrats. Turnout there is the test of whether her move costs El-Sayed anything. Celinda Lake, a strategist working with El-Sayed's campaign, expects a "really close" race that hinges on turnout. Michigan is one of the seats that could decide Senate control.

Iran: negotiators leave Doha with a draft deal in circulation; Tehran offers to reopen Hormuz in two days

Facts

  • Iran's negotiators left Doha on Sunday. An official told Reuters the departure wasn't due to problems and that a draft proposal for a US deal is circulating, with a follow-up meeting possible. Deputy FM Kazem Gharibabadi relayed messages to Washington through Qatar.
  • FM Abbas Araqchi told Al Jazeera the Strait of Hormuz could reopen within two days of a deal, down from Iran's earlier seven-day plan. Spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said nothing reopens unless the US naval blockade ends and Iran's other demands are met. Qatar's Al-Araby Al-Jadeed reports preliminary understandings on nuclear issues, shipping and financial incentives, with China and Russia as guarantors.
  • Trump says there will be no new attacks before the vote and that the US is "not in that much of a hurry." In Dubuque he said "at some point we're going to have to give them the hammer." Per Axios, Netanyahu pressed him in a "tough" call Saturday to strike right after the midterms. Trump described the call as "very good."
  • UKMTO reported a tanker hit by two unknown projectiles off Saudi Arabia, with all crew safe.

Left view

NPR, reporting from Bahrain, focuses on Gulf anxiety. It questions the administration's line that Iran is "essentially finished," noting that the Revolutionary Guard can still hit Gulf states and has threatened undersea internet cables. The Guardian reported the two-day timetable as agreed. The Times of Israel called it conditional, a gap worth noting.

Right view

Fox News's live coverage stresses Trump's leverage: Iran's navy and air force are largely destroyed, and only "one or two" nuclear sites are left. It treats fuel prices tied to the war as a midterm liability that renewed strikes could reshape. The Washington Examiner has argued that operational signals point toward more strikes regardless of the talks.

Watch for

Forbes's risk checklist puts Hormuz traffic at roughly two transits a day against a pre-war average of 138. War-risk insurance runs 6-10% of hull value, against about 0.25% before the war. A deal that ends the blockade would be the single biggest downside catalyst for oil. A post-election strike would likely send Brent back toward its October highs. Iran's army says any new round would last longer than the last.

Alaska: judge tosses DOJ bid to block Senate certification; Sullivan says he'll accept the result

Facts

  • US District Judge Sharon Gleason dismissed the Justice Department's suit on Sunday in a 20-page order. The suit sought to delay certification if GOP Sen. Dan S. Sullivan loses. She ruled DOJ lacked standing because it couldn't show a court order would fix its alleged injury. She added that it offered no "workable plan" to isolate ballots cast for the second "Daniel J. Sullivan" without breaking ballot secrecy.
  • DOJ says it will appeal to the 9th Circuit. Separately, it is running a criminal probe into how Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. got on the ballot and has subpoenaed several Democratic officials. No one has been charged.
  • Sen. Sullivan told CNN he'll accept the results but accused Mary Peltola's camp of cheating. He wouldn't say whether he'd back a refusal to certify, which Gov. Mike Dunleavy has floated. Peltola denies any role and calls the fight a distraction from high prices.

Left view

NPR, MS NOW and the Anchorage Daily News headline it as Trump's attempt to block results being rejected. MS NOW stood out for highlighting Sullivan's pledge to accept the outcome. Peltola's campaign frames the lawsuit as a pre-emptive challenge to a race Republicans fear losing.

Right view

Fox News stresses that the case was dismissed on procedural grounds without reaching DOJ's core claim. It leans on the New York Times report that people tied to Peltola's campaign helped recruit the spoiler, and gives prominence to the appeal and criminal probe. Newsweek's headline emphasizes that the judge was Obama-appointed.

Watch for

Alaska's ranked-choice tally means a result could take days or weeks. If Peltola wins narrowly, expect a 9th Circuit appeal and a fight over whether Dunleavy certifies. That could leave a seat in limbo during the battle for Senate control.

Israel: officials say election results stand despite Likud's "stolen" claim; certification due this week

Facts

  • Netanyahu's bloc has 53 seats and Gadi Eisenkot's opposition bloc 48, so neither has a majority in the 120-seat Knesset. Arab-led parties won a record 19 seats. Eisenkot's bloc plus the Arab parties would reach 67.
  • Likud alleges, without public evidence, that Arab parties "stole" the vote, including with Qatari money. Qatar calls it disinformation. Central Elections Committee chair Noam Sohlberg said in a joint video with the Shin Bet chief and attorney general that even if the suspicions are borne out, they wouldn't undermine the result.
  • Police arrested three activists allegedly carrying cash to bribe voters and linked them to Ra'am. Two were released Sunday, one under house arrest. Ra'am leader Mansour Abbas called the arrests undemocratic.

Left view

Al Jazeera and the Times of Israel present the fraud claim as groundwork to delegitimize a possible Jewish-Arab government. The Times of Israel argues Netanyahu may be positioning for a repeat election in which Arab turnout drops. Eisenkot accuses him of a coordinated campaign to discredit the results.

Right view

The Jerusalem Post's coverage relays Likud's case: hundreds of testimonies, cash-filled envelopes tied to Ra'am, and banned voter-mobilization software, which Likud calls a "grave blow to Israeli democracy." The Post notes the allegations are unverified. US right-leaning coverage of the dispute is thin, and Israeli right-wing parties are making the case themselves.

Watch for

Once results are certified, President Isaac Herzog nominates someone to try to form a government. Watch whether Eisenkot drops his earlier refusal to partner with Arab parties. That decision affects Israel's posture toward any renewed US strikes on Iran.
