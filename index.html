Hormuz standoff drags on as oil spikes and a US–Iran deal slips further away

Facts

  • Oil jumped roughly 5% Monday as doubts grew that Washington and Tehran will strike a deal to restore commercial traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, per CNBC and Reuters.
  • Shipping through the strait — normally about a fifth of global oil supply — has effectively collapsed since the conflict reignited; MarineTraffic counted 8–15 transits a day early this month versus roughly 130 before the war.
  • Iran's Supreme National Security Council says reopening requires the US to lift its naval blockade and sanctions, withdraw regional forces, pay reparations, and release frozen assets — terms Washington rejects.
  • Iran and Oman have reportedly agreed on coordinates for a proposed safe shipping corridor, but no deal is finalized.

Left view

NPR and CNN frame this as a self-inflicted crisis: a CNN analysis likens Trump's predicament to Jimmy Carter's, with a stalled economy and rising prices heading into the midterms. Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA) argues the US went from a bad deal to renewed strikes, another blockade, and turmoil that only pushes prices higher, and Democrats are pressing to reassert congressional war powers.

Right view

Fox News highlights Trump's ultimatum framing — reopen the strait or face military action — and Rep. Mike Turner (R-OH) argues the strait can be reopened without a ground war. But the coalition isn't uniform: Republicans Rand Paul, Susan Collins and Lisa Murkowski joined efforts to limit the president's war powers.

Watch for

Analysts tie every headline here directly to crude: sustained disruption keeps Brent elevated and feeds pump prices into the fall. A credible corridor deal could unwind much of the risk premium quickly; a collapse in talks or a fresh tanker incident could spike oil further and complicate the Fed's rate path.

Trump extends China tariff pause another 90 days, hours before truce expired

Facts

  • Trump signed an order Tuesday extending the pause on a scheduled hike of reciprocal tariffs on China to 125%, keeping the current truce in place for roughly another 90 days, per PBS NewsHour, NBC News and CBS News.
  • The extension preserves the arrangement in place since May, under which the US charges about 30% and China about 10% while negotiators keep working.
  • It's the second such extension of the year; the prior truce was set to lapse this week.

Left view

PBS NewsHour and other mainstream outlets stress the whiplash of governing by deadline: repeated 90-day pauses leave importers and manufacturers unable to plan, and the underlying tariffs still raise costs that the Tax Foundation estimates fall largely on US buyers.

Right view

Trump and supportive outlets cast the extension as leverage working — buying time for a broader deal with Xi while keeping the threat of 125% duties on the table. Trump said he takes Beijing "at their word" on commitments made so far.

Watch for

Markets welcomed the reprieve — equity futures firmed — but economists warn the reprieve is temporary and the next cliff lands in the fall. Watch whether talks produce anything durable on rare earths, agriculture and tech export controls before the clock runs out again.

Weak July jobs report reshapes the Fed's September calculus

Facts

  • US employers cut 23,000 jobs in July versus a forecast gain of roughly 80,000 — a sharp miss, per CNBC and Fox Business.
  • Prior months were revised down hard: May cut to 63,000 from 129,000; June to 20,000 from 57,000.
  • Wage growth slowed to 3.2% year over year, the weakest since 2021; labor-force participation fell to 61.4%, a multi-year low.
  • Odds of a September rate hike fell from about 57% to 44%; next week's inflation data is seen as decisive.

Left view

Yahoo Finance and NPR-adjacent coverage read the data as evidence the labor market is cracking under the weight of tariffs and the Iran-driven energy shock, arguing the Fed should hold or ease rather than risk tipping the economy into recession before the midterms.

Right view

Fox Business and market commentators note the twist: soft jobs plus tariff-driven price pressure raises the specter of stagflation, and some argue the Fed shouldn't cut prematurely with inflation risks from oil still live. The data cut hike odds but didn't take a hike fully off the table.

Watch for

Barclays and Goldman now flag a possible cut path; all eyes are on next week's CPI. A hot inflation print alongside weak jobs would sharpen the stagflation debate and could whipsaw both bonds and equities.

Ukraine leans on long-range drones as Zelensky presses for US air defense

Facts

  • Ukraine has hit Russian oil facilities with long-range drones on a near-daily basis in recent months, contributing to fuel shortages inside Russia, per NPR and Reuters.
  • Russia said it downed 605 Ukrainian drones over its territory and Crimea around Aug. 6; Russian strikes on eastern Ukraine killed at least six overnight that day, per Al Jazeera.
  • Zelensky has appealed for additional US air-defense missiles; US-mediated talks have not produced a ceasefire, with the sides split over territory and security guarantees.

Left view

The Guardian and NPR emphasize Ukraine's civilian toll from Russian barrages and frame continued Western air-defense support as essential; a Ukrainian negotiator argues sustained drone strikes on Russian energy could force Moscow toward a ceasefire.

Right view

Right-leaning outlets are more divided: some back arming Ukraine as cost-effective pressure on Moscow, while restraint-minded voices question open-ended commitments amid the Middle East war and domestic economic strain, pressing for a negotiated settlement.

Watch for

Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries add a second geopolitical bid to oil markets already jittery over Hormuz. Whether the US ships more interceptors — and whether either capital moves on territory — will shape both the battlefield and energy prices.
