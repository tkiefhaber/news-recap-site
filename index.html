25 states sue over Trump's new tariffs, calling them a workaround of a Supreme Court loss

Facts

  • A coalition of 25 Democratic-led states sued the administration Monday in the US Court of International Trade over tariffs of 10% or 12.5% on goods from about 60 trading partners.
  • The new duties were imposed last month under Section 301 of the Trade Act, with the administration citing other nations' failure to curb forced-labor imports.
  • The suit calls the tariffs "arbitrary, capricious, and contrary to law" and a pretext to revive levies the Supreme Court struck down in February under the emergency-powers statute (IEEPA).
  • The states ask the court to block the tariffs and refund duties paid; the White House says the measures are a lawful response to unfair trade practices.

Left view

Outlets like PBS NewsHour and TIME amplify the states' argument that this is a repackaging of illegal tariffs that raise costs on families. New York AG Letitia James and California AG Rob Bonta framed it as the administration "breaking law after law" to hike prices after losing at the Supreme Court.

Right view

Coverage sympathetic to the administration (via the White House response carried by CNBC and Al Jazeera) stresses that Section 301 is a long-standing, distinct legal authority for addressing unfair trade and forced labor, and that using it is well within executive power — casting the suit as partisan lawfare by blue states.

Watch for

The Court of International Trade's handling could set up another Supreme Court fight over presidential trade authority. Importers and retailers face continued cost uncertainty; a ruling either way ripples into consumer prices, inflation data, and Fed rate expectations heading into the fall.

Congo's Ebola outbreak becomes the country's largest on record

Facts

  • As of Aug. 3–4, the DRC reported roughly 3,874 confirmed cases and about 1,751 related deaths, per WHO figures — the largest Ebola outbreak in the country's history.
  • Caused by the Bundibugyo strain, the outbreak has spread from Ituri to five provinces and 49 health zones in under three months.
  • Existing certified Ebola treatments target the Zaire strain, complicating the response; Canadian officials have approved clinical trials of a Bundibugyo-targeted vaccine.
  • The UN opened a new treatment center at the outbreak's center as case counts continued to rise.

Left view

Bloomberg and UN News stress the humanitarian scale and strained global health financing, implicitly raising concerns about cuts to aid and pandemic-preparedness funding leaving front-line responders under-resourced against a fast-moving strain.

Right view

Mainstream-right coverage of the outbreak is thin; where addressed, the framing tends toward border and travel-screening vigilance and skepticism of large open-ended international aid commitments, favoring targeted, accountable spending over blanket funding.

Watch for

Public-health experts warn a Bundibugyo-strain outbreak spreading across provinces raises cross-border risk into Uganda and beyond. Watch WHO's emergency classification, vaccine-trial timelines, and any travel-screening steps at international hubs.
