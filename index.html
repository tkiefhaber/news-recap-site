Trump calls off Iran strike; direct talks set to begin today

Facts

  • Trump announced Saturday night he had canceled a planned U.S.–Israeli strike on Iran, saying the "parameters of a deal" had been agreed to. It would have been one of the largest bombing campaigns since WWII, aimed at Iranian energy infrastructure.
  • He said he held off after Gulf allies — Saudi Arabia, Qatar and the UAE — urged restraint, and made it conditional on being able to reach a deal quickly. U.S. forces remain on full alert.
  • Proposed terms center on the immediate, full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and an end to Iran's nuclear threat.
  • Direct U.S.–Iran negotiations are set to begin Monday, per the administration.

Left view

NPR and CNN framed the reversal as a relief and an off-ramp, while flagging that the administration is releasing less information about a war it never got congressional authorization for. NPR noted skepticism about whether any deal holds given repeated collapses since June. MSNBC's Chris Matthews praised Trump for hesitating on the strike.

Right view

National Review characterized the shift as a retreat — from earlier demands of "unconditional surrender" down to "Strait of Hormuz bargaining." Fox News's Dana Perino noted the war handed Iran leverage over Hormuz it didn't have before hostilities, and Fox reported Tehran spinning the framework as a victory over the U.S. and Israel. Radio host Hugh Hewitt mocked the announced deal.

Watch for

Oil is the swing factor: WTI sat near $85 and Brent near $90 late last week on Hormuz risk. A durable reopening could pull crude and pump prices lower; a breakdown in talks could send oil back above $95. Analysts also see the outcome shaping the political mood three months out from the midterms.

Wildfires tear through Spokane; 60,000 evacuated, hundreds of homes lost

Facts

  • Three fast-moving fires — Old Trails, Fairview and Meadowview — broke out Saturday near Spokane, Wash., and jumped the Spokane River into the city, driven by high winds and heat.
  • Roughly 60,000 people were placed under evacuation orders; at least 600 homes, businesses and other structures were destroyed. The fires burned more than 7,000 acres with 0% containment.
  • Gov. Bob Ferguson declared a state of emergency and activated the National Guard; the National Weather Service issued a "particularly dangerous situation" red flag warning for eastern Washington.
  • Officials reported no confirmed injuries as of early in the response, while cautioning it was still preliminary.

Left view

NPR and The Guardian tie the fire's speed and intensity to a hotter, drier climate lengthening the Western fire season, and emphasize evacuee support and preparedness funding.

Right view

Fox 13 Seattle and local outlets like KHQ foreground forest and brush management, evacuation logistics and the National Guard response, with less emphasis on climate framing.

Watch for

Containment hinges on winds easing and temperatures dropping midweek. Expect large rebuilding and insurance costs in the Spokane area and degraded air quality across the Inland Northwest.

Deadly migrant surge into Spain's Ceuta strains EU border politics

Facts

  • Close to 50,000–60,000 migrants rushed from Morocco into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta beginning July 30, many swimming around a sea barrier.
  • The death toll reached at least 57 on the Spanish side — some drowned, others crushed against the breakwater; some counts ran higher.
  • Spain deployed the military to assist the Civil Guard; Ceuta's president declared a humanitarian emergency, with migrants, including unaccompanied children, sleeping in parks. More than 48,000 had returned to Morocco by Friday.
  • There were clashes in the Moroccan border town of Bni Nsar, where police vehicles were set ablaze.

Left view

Al Jazeera and NPR emphasize the humanitarian toll, the deaths of children and questions about Morocco's role in allowing the surge, calling for safe processing over militarized pushbacks.

Right view

Right-leaning European and U.S. commentators cite the episode as evidence of porous EU borders and leverage-driven migration, arguing for tighter enforcement and pressure on Rabat.

Watch for

Whether Madrid and Rabat strike a new border-management deal, and how the crisis feeds Europe's broader immigration debate — a recurring flashpoint that U.S. outlets increasingly map onto domestic politics.
