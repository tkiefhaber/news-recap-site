Facts

Ukraine says its naval drones sank a Russian warship off Libya; Russia claims it downed 1,246 drones overnight

Facts

These are Russian figures and have not been independently verified.

Left view

Europe-based coverage such as RTÉ's Reuters report leads with the strain on the alliance: Kyiv acted alone in a theater where Italy has direct security interests. Pro-Ukraine outlets present the Libya strike and the scale of the Moscow barrage as proof that Ukraine can still hit back while talks stall.

Right view

Right-leaning US outlets this week have focused on Trump's push for a deal by December or January rather than on the Libya strike. US coverage of the Mediterranean operation, across the spectrum, was thin as of Saturday morning.

Watch for

Whether Rome and other EU capitals push back publicly on the Mediterranean strike, and how Moscow retaliates. Zelenskyy has warned that a massive strike on the power grid is coming. The mid-December Miami G20 is still the target for a framework deal.

Second judge blocks Trump's new birthright-citizenship order

Facts

  • On Nov 25, US District Judge Christopher Cooper preliminarily blocked Trump's second executive order on birthright citizenship. It would have denied automatic citizenship to children of foreign government workers, children born through commercial surrogacy, and children of suspected gang members (American Tribune, UPI).
  • The ACLU represents the parents, who sued in September. The ruling builds on the Supreme Court's June 2026 decision upholding birthright citizenship. The Hill reports Cooper is the second judge to block the new order.
  • A White House spokeswoman dismissed it as another ruling from an Obama-appointed "activist judge." The ACLU's Cody Wofsy said the third attempt fares no better than the first two.

Left view

MS NOW and left-leaning headlines syndicated on Yahoo frame the ruling as the judge throwing the Supreme Court's own decision back at Trump. Their argument is that a president cannot carve new exceptions into the 14th Amendment by executive order. The ACLU calls the order a rerun of an argument that has already lost.

Right view

American Tribune echoes the White House "activist judge" line and argues that the blocked categories are narrow abuses that should be closed. Breitbart ran the wire story without extra framing.

Watch for

An appeal is likely. The June Supreme Court precedent will weigh heavily on any reversal attempt. The issue could come back in January if the Democratic House moves to put birthright protections into statute.

Indiana's United Fidelity Bank fails in the largest US bank collapse since 2024

Facts

  • The OCC closed United Fidelity Bank of Evansville, Indiana, on Friday and named the FDIC receiver. First Citizens takes on substantially all deposits and assets, about $4.5B of deposits. Some brokered accounts were excluded (FDIC, Startup Fortune).
  • The bank had $5.9B in assets and 22 branches in IN, IL, FL, CO and the USVI. The FDIC estimates a $1.83B cost to the Deposit Insurance Fund. It is the seventh US bank failure this year.
  • Nonaccrual loans jumped from $16M in June to $2.16B in September, mostly in a multifamily book that made up $2.72B of its $3.06B in loans. Its core capital ratio fell from 11% to 4.7%.

Left view

Startup Fortune's analysis points the finger at oversight: an OCC consent order flagged the bank's weak spots in 2023, years before the loans went bad. Consumer coverage notes that insured depositors are protected but holders of brokered CDs face delays. Partisan outlets on both sides have largely skipped the story.

Right view

Money Morning's market-oriented coverage stresses that this was a known problem bank already on regulators' watch list. That framing treats it as a case of concentrated real-estate risk rather than a sign of wider trouble.

Watch for

Investors will look hard at other lenders heavy in multifamily real estate, with the 10-year yield near 4.9% and a Fed hike expected Dec 9. Regional bank stocks trade again Monday.
