Iran–US near deal to reopen Strait of Hormuz; oil and stocks react

Facts

  • Trump says a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and pursue Iranian denuclearization is "imminent," with reopening framed as "phase one" and denuclearization to follow. Tehran has publicly denied holding direct talks.
  • Qatar said an interim proposal has been drafted; Oman and Pakistan are also mediating. Both sides signaled progress this week.
  • Treasury's Scott Bessent publicly signaled a Hormuz deal is in sight, per Benzinga. The strait carries roughly a quarter of seaborne oil trade and was declared "closed" by Iran back in March.
  • Markets rallied: the S&P 500 and Dow closed at record highs Tuesday (S&P +1.8% to 7,737; Dow +907 to 54,086; Nasdaq 100 +3.3%). Brent fell to about $83.72 and WTI to about $75.19 on the news.

Left view

Democrats are largely content to watch the GOP fight over the deal, framing it (per The Hill's reporting of Democratic aides) as Trump escaping a "reckless and unnecessary war" he started, now willing to pay almost any price to exit. Mainstream outlets (CBS News, Washington Post) emphasize the secrecy around the deal text.

Right view

Hawkish conservatives are the loudest critics. National Review's Philip Klein demanded the White House "release the text"; Ted Cruz warned an outcome that leaves Iran with cash, enrichment, and leverage over Hormuz would be a "disastrous mistake" (Fox News); Ben Shapiro called it a "disaster." Trump lashed out at "fools" opposing the deal (CBS News).

Watch for

Analysts (Bloomberg, Schwab) tie further oil downside and equity gains to actual reopening — a signed, verifiable interim deal could push Brent lower still and feed into pump prices within weeks; collapse of talks would spike crude and revive strike risk. Denuclearization "phase two" is the harder, slower fight.

Blanche AG nomination clears committee after fund standoff

Facts

  • The Senate Judiciary Committee voted 12-10 along party lines to advance Todd Blanche, Trump's former defense lawyer and acting AG, to the full Senate.
  • GOP holdouts Cornyn and Tillis backed him after securing a deal that clarified Trump's IRS immunity arrangement and rescinded the administration's roughly $1.8 billion "Anti-Weaponization Fund."
  • Sen. Susan Collins (R) said she will vote "no," citing erosion of DOJ independence. With all Democrats expected to oppose, no more than three Republicans can defect for confirmation.

Left view

Democrats (per NBC News, CBS News) and Judiciary Democrats warn Blanche has further politicized the department and that the "anti-weaponization" fund was itself a weaponization tool; they cast the confirmation fight as a test of DOJ independence.

Right view

Most Republicans defend Blanche as a capable lawyer and argue the negotiated concessions resolved legitimate concerns (Washington Post). Collins's defection is treated as a Maine-moderate outlier rather than a broader GOP revolt.

Watch for

A floor vote in the coming days with a razor-thin margin. Whip-counting Collins plus any additional GOP wavering (Murkowski) will decide it; confirmation would cement Trump's control of DOJ leadership heading into the midterms.

DR Congo Ebola outbreak becomes country's largest ever

Facts

  • The Bundibugyo-strain Ebola outbreak in DR Congo has killed more than 1,700 people out of roughly 3,800 recorded cases, per government figures reported by Al Jazeera.
  • Declared May 15 in Ituri Province, it is now the DRC's largest and fastest-spreading Ebola outbreak on record.
  • The WHO says it is accelerating vaccine and treatment trials as the response struggles to keep pace.

Left view

Left-leaning and humanitarian-focused outlets emphasize global health funding gaps and argue wealthy nations, including the US, should scale up support for WHO-led containment and vaccine access.

Right view

Right-leaning coverage is thinner; where present, it stresses border screening, questions the effectiveness of multilateral bodies, and scrutinizes how US aid dollars are spent — mainstream US coverage is leading here.

Watch for

Whether accelerated trials and international logistics slow the spread before it crosses more widely into Uganda. Experts warn a Bundibugyo strain complicates vaccine strategies tuned to the Zaire strain.
