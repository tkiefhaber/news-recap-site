The Big Stories Today

US readies new tariffs on China, the EU and 14 others, a day after a New York jury convicts Huawei

Facts

  • The administration is expected to announce higher tariffs on 16 economies, including China and the EU, in the coming days. The trigger is USTR Jamieson Greer's Section 301 probe of subsidized "excess capacity." The WSJ and Bloomberg broke the story Tuesday, and AP confirmed it through two people familiar with the plan. Timing could still slip.
  • Per the WSJ (via 10 Things), China would face rates of up to 20%, while the EU, Japan and South Korea would pay 15% or less. These come on top of the 10%–12.5% forced-labor tariffs from July, which are already in court. Together they replace the April 2025 duties the Supreme Court struck down in February.
  • A federal jury in New York convicted Huawei Tuesday on 10 of 12 counts, including bank fraud, conspiracy to steal trade secrets and lying about Iran dealings. It acquitted the company of racketeering conspiracy and obstruction. Huawei says the verdict has no legal or factual merit and plans to appeal (Reuters, AFP).
  • Beijing said it "firmly opposes" US suppression of Chinese firms and called the overcapacity charge a pretext. The news comes days after Trump and Xi praised ties at APEC in Shenzhen. Xi is due at Trump's G20 at Doral next month, and the trade truce expires in January (AFP, Asia Financial).

Left view

AP points out that the move follows a midterm rout that it largely attributes to affordability anger. It frames the tariffs as an early test for incoming Democratic majorities, which can pass bills against them but cannot override a veto. Democratic coverage focuses on prices: new duties land just as voters punished the GOP over the cost of living. The Huawei verdict itself draws little criticism from the left.

Right view

The administration argues that foreign subsidies let overseas producers undercut US manufacturers, and it presents Section 301 as a legally sturdier route after the Supreme Court ruling (WSJ). On Huawei, AG Todd Blanche said no company is above the law. China hawks see the verdict as overdue vindication. The free-market right, for example Reason and the WSJ editorial page in past rounds, has long warned that tariffs are a tax on US buyers. Fresh right-of-center commentary on this round is thin so far.

Watch for

The USTR report and actual rates. Expect legal challenges (10 Things) and possible EU and Chinese retaliation before the January truce deadline. Huawei sentencing is set for September 2027. Tariff headlines added to Tuesday's chip selloff, and importers of Asian and EU goods face higher costs heading into 2027.

Trump calls a Huang pardon "always an option" as Nvidia gives back part of Monday's rally

Facts

  • In an Oval Office interview with the NY Post, Trump said a pardon for Jensen Huang is "always an option" but probably won't be needed. He also said Huang should answer Congress rather than take the Fifth (Daily Caller, Newsweek).
  • AG Todd Blanche said DOJ has no suspected wrongdoing on file but couldn't predict future investigations. Reuters reports Huang told staff he broke parts of Nvidia's code of conduct and made "terrible mistakes."
  • Huang sits for a closed-door interview with three House panels on Dec. 1 (CBS) and testifies publicly to Senate Banking on Dec. 3.
  • Nvidia fell about 5.9% Tuesday after Monday's record 31% gain. The Philadelphia chip index dropped 7.9% (GSM/CNBC, Motley Fool).

Left view

Newsweek and CNN put the pardon talk next to DOJ's insistence that nothing is under investigation, which raises the question of what a pardon would be for. Schumer promised Democrats will investigate. A House Intelligence spokesperson said the reinstatement "changes nothing" (via CBS). Newsweek notes that some Republicans have also criticized Trump's role.

Right view

The Daily Caller and Daily Wire ran it straight and led with Trump's view that a pardon isn't necessary. They highlighted his line that he cares about chips, not the affair. Pro-Trump outlets see Huang's return as a win for US AI leadership. The GOP senators who objected Monday (Ricketts, Cramer, Curtis) haven't responded publicly to the pardon remark.

Watch for

Whether Huang invokes the Fifth on Dec. 1–3, and whether Democrats subpoena what officials told Nvidia's board. Chip stocks are now trading on headlines. Combined with the new China tariffs, watch for any move on export controls.

Foxx concedes after recount, giving Democrats 258 House seats, the most for either party since 1992

Facts

  • Democrat Chuck Hubbard ousted 11-term GOP Rep. Virginia Foxx, chair of House Rules, in North Carolina by about 330 votes after a recount. Democrats have now gained 44 seats, against 40 in 2018 (CBS/AP, Fox News).
  • Republicans hold at least 173 seats. Still open: a Minnesota recount set for Nov. 30, a Tennessee seat the incumbent has conceded but AP hasn't called, and two Louisiana runoffs in December. Senate Democrats hold 56 seats (CBS, Daily Kos).
  • Hakeem Jeffries is expected to become speaker on Jan. 3. He would be the first Black speaker, and with this margin he could lose dozens of votes and still win. Jim Jordan will lead House Republicans (CBS).

Left view

Daily Kos celebrated the result and said NC Republicans "dummymandered" their mid-decade map. It counts Foxx as the 26th House Republican who voted to overturn 2020 to lose this cycle. Jeffries says Democrats have the century's largest majority. CBS reports a 100-day agenda: reverse GOP Medicaid cuts, restore ACA credits and launch investigations. Rep. Tom Suozzi wants the split to favor affordability over accountability by 75/25.

Right view

Fox News called it a historic Democratic comeback that reshapes Washington. Republicans are focused on what comes next: the White House warned the planned probes could amount to "blatant criminality," and Jordan vowed the GOP will "fight for American families" (CBS). Trump has pointed to his veto pen.

Watch for

Dec. 11 funding fight in the lame duck, then January oversight subpoenas and whether Jeffries keeps impeachment off the table. Divided government means gridlock on tax and trade. Markets usually shrug off gridlock, but the tariffs above are an early test.

Houthis hit Saudi wealth fund's Riyadh tower a second time and threaten Red Sea ports

Facts

  • Houthis struck the Public Investment Fund's headquarters in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District for the second time in under a week, injuring 11. Earlier this month they hit an Aramco-owned building (Al Jazeera, WSJ via 10 Things).
  • Coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki promised a firm response, and the GCC condemned the attack. Most international flights to Riyadh were reportedly canceled (10 Things).
  • The Houthis say they will target Jeddah and every Saudi Red Sea port if Riyadh attacks Hodeidah (Reuters). Separately, Iran's first VP said Tehran isn't worried about the US naval blockade (Reuters).

Left view

International and center-left outlets such as Al Jazeera and the Guardian frame it as spillover from the US–Iran war: the conflict is now reaching Gulf financial centers, and the risk to civilians and aviation is growing.

Right view

Right-leaning US outlets treat it mostly as wire copy. Hawks cite the strikes as proof that Iran's proxies must be confronted and that the blockade should stay in place. US mainstream coverage overall is thin; Gulf and international outlets are leading.

Watch for

A Saudi strike on Hodeidah, and whether the Houthis follow through against Jeddah or Red Sea shipping. That would threaten Saudi export routes that bypass Hormuz. Oil fell to a three-month low on Monday, so a Red Sea shock isn't priced in.

Venezuela discloses $237.5B in public debt ahead of one of history's largest sovereign restructurings

Facts

  • President Delcy Rodríguez's US-backed government put total public debt at $237.5B, about 242% of GDP. That includes about $110B in defaulted bonds and unpaid interest, more than $26B in arbitration awards and $67.3B in non-financial debt, mostly PDVSA's (FT, Bloomberg via 10 Things).
  • China and Russia hold about $22B in official claims. Inflation is forecast above 300% by year-end. The government projects $160B of oil investment over a decade.

Left view

Left-leaning coverage focuses on the human cost of hyperinflation and asks whether a US-backed government can make bondholders take losses without new austerity.

Right view

Right-leaning and market-friendly outlets (WSJ, Bloomberg) see an opening for US creditors and oil majors now that the Maduro era is over. They note that Chinese and Russian claims will test whose loans get priority. Coverage is mostly financial press; general political coverage is thin on both sides.

Watch for

Bondholder committee terms and haircut size, and whether Beijing and Moscow get equal treatment. Also watch Venezuelan oil output: a credible plan would add barrels and weigh on crude.
