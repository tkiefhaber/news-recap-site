US–Iran war flares again as strikes spread across the region

Facts

  • US forces completed a heavy wave of strikes on southern Iran Wednesday night — explosions reported around Bandar Abbas, Kish and the Persian Gulf — after Iran's Revolutionary Guard fired ballistic missiles at US positions.
  • Iran retaliated Thursday, hitting a building in Kuwait (one worker killed); Jordan said it intercepted five missiles. Saudi Arabia struck Houthi targets in Yemen after a Red Sea attack on a Saudi vessel.
  • The bombing campaign has run for roughly two weeks straight after a June ceasefire collapsed; the broader conflict is now in its fifth month. Defense Secretary Hegseth has put the war's cost near $37.5B.
  • Trump said it was "our turn" to hit Iran hard and threatened Iranian infrastructure (bridges, power plants) over Strait of Hormuz attacks. Oman and Iran are reportedly holding talks over the strait.

Left view

The Guardian and MSNBC frame an open-ended war with no congressional authorization and rising US casualties as a constitutional and strategic crisis, noting European allies and Democratic leaders want an off-ramp. PBS NewsHour highlights growing anti-interventionist anger even inside Trump's own base.

Right view

Coverage on the right is unusually split. A Fox News poll found 56% oppose the strikes and Trump's disapproval on Iran hit a record 66%; Laura Ingraham and other Fox voices are urging de-escalation before the midterms, and National Review declared "the American people are done with war." Tucker Carlson, Megyn Kelly and Matt Walsh have broken sharply with Trump, while the administration argues the campaign is a necessary "detour" to keep nuclear weapons out of Iran's hands.

Watch for

Analysts tie the war directly to energy prices: any real disruption to Hormuz traffic could push oil back toward the $100+ levels seen July 23. Watch the Oman–Iran talks, tanker transit counts (14 crossings Wednesday, up from single digits), and whether GOP nervousness forces a War Powers vote. Markets remain jumpy on every escalation headline.

Trump announces "historic" deal for Hamas to disarm in Gaza

Facts

  • Trump said Thursday his "Board of Peace" — with Egypt, Qatar and Turkey as mediators — reached a deal for Hamas to fully disarm and Israel to withdraw from Gaza, paving the way for a new technocratic Palestinian administration.
  • The framework requires a detailed disarmament roadmap within 14 days and a series of reciprocal, independently verified steps over roughly seven to eight months, backed by an International Stabilization Force and a new Palestinian police force.
  • Reports say Hamas signed a disarmament accord in El-Alamein, Egypt on July 30 — the first time it has accepted any weapons framework.
  • Neither Israel nor Hamas fully endorsed it: Netanyahu says it falls short of "complete demilitarization," and Palestinian Islamic Jihad called the reports "inaccurate."

Left view

Outlets like NPR and Axios treat it as a potentially real breakthrough but lead with the caveats — vague timelines, no firm mechanism to disarm PIJ, and the risk Trump is overselling an unfinished agreement. Emphasis on the humanitarian stakes and whether a credible Palestinian governing body can actually stand up.

Right view

Fox News frames Hamas as "the ultimate spoiler" and echoes Israeli intelligence distrust of Qatar and Turkey, warning the group may be buying a tactical reprieve rather than genuinely disarming. The Jerusalem Post stresses Israel's skepticism that the 15-point plan meets its security bar.

Watch for

The 14-day roadmap deadline is the first real test. Watch whether Israel formally signs on, whether Gaza police actually hand over weapons in the next two weeks, and how PIJ responds. A durable deal would ease regional risk premiums; a collapse would reinforce skepticism and keep Gaza a flashpoint.

Divided Fed holds rates steady for a fifth straight meeting

Facts

  • The FOMC voted 9–3 on July 29 to hold the federal funds rate at 3.5%–3.75%, its lowest since November 2022, for the fifth meeting running.
  • Three regional presidents — Hammack (Cleveland), Kashkari (Minneapolis) and Logan (Dallas) — dissented, citing inflation stuck above the 2% target for over five years.
  • Chair Kevin Warsh declined to call it a "pause," describing it as a "rigorous review" of the economy, which the Fed says is expanding at a solid pace despite Middle East uncertainty.

Left view

Left-leaning economists worry a Fed holding at multi-year lows while inflation runs hot risks eroding household purchasing power, and question whether political pressure is shaping a dovish stance.

Right view

Fox Business and market conservatives highlight the three dissents as evidence the Fed is behind the curve on persistent inflation, and press Warsh to prove independence from the White House.

Watch for

With war-driven oil feeding into prices, the inflation path is the key variable. Watch the next CPI print and whether dissents grow — a hotter reading could force the "rigorous review" toward a hike, pressuring both stocks and mortgage rates.

Deadly migrant surge overwhelms Spain's Ceuta enclave

Facts

  • At least 18 migrants died — many drowned, some in a crush at the Tarajal breakwater — after thousands breached the Morocco border into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta.
  • Spain's Interior Ministry says roughly 49,000 people crossed in 24 hours, overwhelming reception centers and leaving many, including unaccompanied children, sleeping in streets and parks.
  • Spain deployed the military to support the Civil Guard; PM Pedro Sánchez was set to visit Ceuta Friday. Madrid has accused Morocco of "blackmail" over the surge.

Left view

The Guardian and NPR foreground the humanitarian catastrophe — deaths, stranded children, overwhelmed shelters — and scrutinize both Morocco's apparent use of migration as leverage and Europe's border-security response.

Right view

Right-leaning European and US outlets cast it as proof of border fragility and the risks of weaponized migration, backing Spain's military deployment and pressing for firmer enforcement and pushback against Rabat.

Watch for

Watch Spain–Morocco diplomacy (aid, Western Sahara leverage), whether the EU intervenes, and if the crisis hardens the bloc's broader migration politics. A repeat could inflame far-right momentum across Europe.
