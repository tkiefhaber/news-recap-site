The Big Stories Today

Massie forces a House vote on impeaching Hegseth, and it could come by Wednesday

Facts

  • Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.), who lost his primary, filed a privileged resolution Monday with eight articles of impeachment against War Secretary Pete Hegseth. Under House rules it must get floor action within two legislative days.
  • The articles cover the Iran war, the Caribbean drug-boat strikes, the Yemen strikes and the Maduro extradition. One article accuses Hegseth of retaliating against Sen. Mark Kelly over his "unlawful orders" video.
  • GOP leaders can move to table the resolution or refer it to committee, which would kill it. Fox News reports some Republicans privately warn it could pass, since members who lost their seats may skip the vote. Retiring Sen. Thom Tillis has backed the effort.
  • Hegseth called the push a "shame" that "won't work." It lands on the first day of a five-week lame-duck session with government funding set to lapse after Dec. 11.

Left view

At The Atlantic, David A. Graham argues that impeaching Cabinet officers is an effective accountability tool and a better route than a presidential impeachment that would go nowhere. NPR and MS NOW frame the vote as a test of how far post-midterm GOP discipline has cracked. Rep. Jamie Raskin (on NBC) said Democrats see impeachment as a tool, not a goal, and will put healthcare first.

Right view

Fox News presents the push as fallout from the Iran war and high gas prices, which Republicans blame for their losses. Rep. Warren Davidson, a war critic, told Fox that impeaching Hegseth would scapegoat him for Congress's own failure to rein in the president. Fox's Chad Pergram warns that once Democrats take over, pressure from the left to impeach "someone" could swamp their affordability message.

Watch for

The tabling vote is the real test. If it fails, impeachment would likely pass the outgoing House, but a Senate conviction needs 67 votes, and the last impeached Cabinet secretary, Alejandro Mayorkas, never got a trial. Massie says he'll drop the effort if Hegseth resigns. Reports that Trump is privately weighing Hegseth's future make a resignation the wildcard.

Republicans pick their post-rout leaders today: Scalise vs. Jordan for House GOP leader

Facts

  • House Republicans hold secret-ballot leadership elections from 1:30 to 5:30 p.m. ET. Speaker Mike Johnson isn't running, so Majority Leader Steve Scalise faces Judiciary Chairman Jim Jordan for the top job in what will be a GOP minority of under 180 members.
  • Down-ballot races: Tom Emmer vs. August Pfluger for whip, a three-way race for conference chair (Lisa McClain, Erin Houchin, Greg Steube), and Brian Jack vs. Rudy Yakym for NRCC chair.
  • Senate Republicans also plan a closed-door vote this morning. The Thune–Barrasso–Cotton team is running again, though some senators want it delayed. The Alaska and Mississippi Senate races are still uncalled.
  • House Democrats vote Nov. 18. Hakeem Jeffries is expected to be unopposed for speaker, which would make him the first Black speaker. The floor vote is Jan. 3.

Left view

MS NOW frames the choice as continuity (Scalise) vs. confrontation (Jordan) for a party still absorbing its defeat. NPR's coverage centers on Jeffries' "America is back" message and Democrats' plans to use their new majorities.

Right view

Right-leaning outlets have put far more attention on the Hegseth fight than on the leadership race, and distinct conservative commentary on Scalise vs. Jordan was thin this morning. Coverage mostly comes from center outlets: Axios and Punchbowl report Scalise pitching steady experience and Jordan pitching a combative, Trump-aligned opposition to a Democratic House.

Watch for

A Jordan win would signal an oversight-war posture toward the incoming Democratic majority. A Scalise win points to a more transactional minority on the Dec. 11 funding deadline, the NDAA and FISA 702. A delay in the Senate GOP vote would show unrest with Thune after the losses.

Trump calls the Iran war "effectively over" but keeps the blockade as the "status quo"

Facts

  • After meeting Qatar's prime minister and VP Vance on Monday, Trump said Iran's military is "effectively over" and that new strikes don't seem necessary. He also said the U.S. stays "locked and loaded."
  • The naval blockade of Iranian ports stays in place, along with the new "Operation Iron Gate" task force to choke off land and Caspian trade. Trump called that the status quo and said Gulf allies prefer it.
  • Qatar's PM called the talks "positive" and reportedly delivered an Iranian proposal. Tehran's terms include lifting sanctions, releasing frozen funds, recognizing a Hormuz shipping route worked out with Oman, and ending Israel's strikes in Lebanon. Rubio called Iran's conditions "outrageous" and doesn't expect an imminent breakthrough.
  • Iranian officials dismissed Trump's remarks as "fake news." President Pezeshkian said Iran is open to talks but won't be coerced.

Left view

The Washington Post and Al Jazeera stress that "effectively over" means an open-ended blockade with no deal, not peace. Critics on the left, such as Daniel Drezner, have argued for months that the war failed its strategic goals. AP coverage notes Trump has said he would "do it again" despite the war's unpopularity and the midterm rebuke.

Right view

Fox News leads with continued pressure: the blockade, Iron Gate, and Trump's "locked and loaded" line, alongside Rubio's dismissal of Tehran's terms. Fox also highlights Trump saying Vance "was right" in his post-election critique of the war. The Washington Times frames Trump as weighing his options after being rebuked by voters.

Watch for

Oil traders are treating the "no new strikes" signal as de-escalation: Brent is down about 12% in a week. But Hormuz traffic depends on a deal Iran ties to sanctions relief. Erdoğan warned Trump against another attack, citing damage to regional economies. A possible Xi–Trump–Putin meeting around APEC in China next week could fold Iran into a bigger bargain.

U.S.–Russia–Ukraine talks open in Abu Dhabi, the first three-way round since February

Facts

  • Two days of talks began Tuesday. Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner lead for the U.S., Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky for Russia, and Rustem Umerov for Ukraine. Security guarantees top the agenda.
  • Fox News reports the U.S. is asking Moscow to accept an "Article Five-like" guarantee for Ukraine modeled on NATO's. Trump hasn't decided what the U.S. itself would commit.
  • Russia still publicly demands all of Donetsk, while Kyiv wants to freeze the current front line. The BBC reports private flexibility, but public positions remain far apart. Rubio said the process has "a ways to go."
  • Overnight, Russia launched 162 drones and a ballistic missile at Ukraine, according to Ukraine's air force. European governments are pushing for a seat despite Russian objections.

Left view

The Guardian led with the overnight Russian strikes on energy infrastructure across 10 regions, framing Moscow as negotiating while it bombs. It largely left out Europe's push to join the talks, which Reuters, the BBC and POLITICO Europe highlighted.

Right view

Fox News frames the round as Trump's team trying to "lock in" safeguards against another invasion. It highlights Kushner's role, the NATO-style guarantee, and the idea of easing sanctions in stages with snap-back if Moscow cheats.

Watch for

Officials don't expect to settle territory in Abu Dhabi. The aim is to map the gaps. The next step could be Witkoff and Kushner meeting Putin and Zelenskyy, and a possible Trump–Putin–Xi encounter at APEC. Any ceasefire talk would weigh further on oil and European gas, which is already down this week.

Huang scandal goes bipartisan: GOP chairs break with Trump and push to tighten chip exports

Facts

  • Nvidia shares fell 4.3% Monday to $178.67, wiping out about $190B in market value, after the board opened a probe into CEO Jensen Huang's affair with a woman U.S. officials call a Chinese spy. The board says it has no reason to believe company secrets were compromised.
  • Per Politico, the GOP chairs of the House Foreign Affairs, Intelligence and China committees asked Huang to a closed-door briefing on Nov. 18. Foreign Affairs chair Brian Mast didn't rule out a subpoena.
  • Trump defended Huang, saying he believes Huang didn't know and that the FBI is looking at "something else."
  • Lawmakers in both parties want to use the NDAA to tighten chip exports, including the bipartisan AI Overwatch Act, which would let Congress review licenses to sell advanced AI chips to China.

Left view

Democrats, including Sens. Elizabeth Warren and Chris Coons, want formal investigations (The Hill, Politico), and Hakeem Jeffries vowed to keep the Senate's China chip limits in the final NDAA. CNN and CNBC focus on Nvidia's close ties to Trump and its lobbying to loosen export rules.

Right view

China hawks on the right are siding with Democrats over Trump. John Moolenaar says Nvidia has "credibility issues," and Jim Banks is pushing the AI Overwatch Act. At The Free Press, Patrick McGee argues the bigger question is whether Huang keeps his outsized sway over China chip policy, and that it would be good if he doesn't.

Watch for

The Nov. 18 briefing and the NDAA conference are the next pressure points. Tighter export rules would cut into Nvidia's China revenue and could hit the wider AI-chip trade. McGee calls Huang "Too Big to Fail, Personified," which points to real key-person risk if the probe turns up more.
