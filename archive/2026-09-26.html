Supreme Court revives SAVE database for voter citizenship checks

Facts

  • In a 6-3 unsigned order, the Supreme Court let DHS resume using the SAVE system with Social Security records to check voters' citizenship status, reversing a June 2026 lower-court order that had paused the agency's 2025 overhaul of the tool.
  • Justices Jackson, Sotomayor and Kagan dissented; the majority cited a 1996 immigration statute letting DHS obtain SSA citizenship data "notwithstanding any other provision" of law, while noting federal law still bars systematic voter-roll purges within 90 days of an election.
  • The administration says the system has checked more than 65 million voters across 26 states and flagged about 28,635 as potential noncitizens.

Left view

NPR reports that critics, including Justice Jackson in dissent, warn the tool can misflag naturalized citizens whose Social Security records are outdated, and that the majority ruled on "questionable interim" grounds without full briefing.

Right view

The Daily Signal and Washington Examiner cast the ruling as a "major victory" for election integrity, emphasizing the majority's statutory reasoning and downplaying the privacy and accuracy objections raised by plaintiffs.

Watch for

Litigation continues in lower courts over how individual states apply the tool ahead of November; voting-rights groups are expected to keep challenging specific flags as misidentifications surface.
