Bipartisan backlash grows over Trump's Russian diesel and sanctions waiver

Facts

  • Trump announced Oct 9 that he would waive energy sanctions to allow purchases of several hundred thousand tons of Russian diesel, citing high energy prices; critics say it conflicts with a 2022 import law and a sanctions law he signed about a month earlier (Roll Call).
  • On Oct 13, six Republicans (including Collins, McConnell, Murkowski, Cornyn and Tillis) and six Democrats urged him to reverse it, calling it a gift to an adversary and a betrayal of allies (Roll Call).
  • Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick (R) plans bipartisan legislation to block Russian oil purchases and may try a discharge petition; Zelensky called the move a “weak decision by strong partners”.
  • Energy experts quoted by Roll Call say the volume would barely move U.S. diesel supply while sending billions of dollars to Russia.

Left view

Democrats cited by Roll Call were uniformly critical, arguing the waiver rewards Moscow and undercuts Ukraine and the law. Al Jazeera and the UK's ITV also highlighted the allied and legal objections.

Right view

Republican leaders defended it: Senate Whip John Barrasso said Trump is balancing the Ukraine war against lowering prices for families, and House Whip Tom Emmer called it thinking “out of the box” (Roll Call). Notably, GOP hawks are among the loudest critics, so this is an intra-right split more than a partisan one.

Watch for

Watch whether a discharge petition or a sanctions bill gets floor time before the election, and whether Russian diesel actually arrives; if not, the tariff threat can be revived. Market impact is modest and mildly bearish for diesel if flows materialize.
