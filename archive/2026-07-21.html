US bombs Iran for a 10th straight night as Houthis declare a Saudi blockade

Facts

Watch for a forced congressional war-powers vote and whether Gulf states pull back basing rights.

Trump slaps 50% tariffs on a new list of Canadian goods

Facts

  • Trump ordered 50% tariffs on a range of Canadian products — including wine, hockey sticks, cement, electrical equipment and machinery — set to take effect in about 30 days.
  • The White House says the move retaliates for Canadian treatment of US alcohol, dairy and auto exports, after most provinces pulled American liquor from shelves and Canada kept quotas on US autos and cheese while sparing the EU.
  • These are among the steepest tariffs the administration has placed on any country.
  • PM Mark Carney called the tariffs a "direct violation" of the USMCA and said Canada is ready to intensify talks.

Left view

CNN Business frames it as a self-inflicted trade war that risks raising prices for US consumers and alienating the closest ally and trading partner, warning the 50% level is a "warning to the rest of the world" that could invite retaliation and dampen growth.

Right view

The Washington Times presents the tariffs as an overdue, proportional response to Canadian boycotts and discriminatory quotas — casting Trump as finally forcing reciprocity after Ottawa singled out American producers while giving Europe a pass.

Watch for

The 30-day runway leaves room for a deal; watch whether Carney offers concessions on dairy and autos or retaliates in kind. Economists flag pass-through to US construction (cement) and manufacturing (machinery, electrical) costs, and the precedent it sets for pending disputes with other USMCA and G7 partners.

Facts

  • The Justice Department hand-delivered grand jury subpoenas to several New York Times reporters seeking phone records, including those of two reporters' spouses.
  • The probe targets sources behind reporting on security shortcomings of the Qatari-gifted Boeing 747 Trump plans to use as Air Force One — including a reported lack of defensive countermeasures.
  • Two subpoenas seek records back to Jan. 1, 2026, well before the July articles, which the Times argues shows the aim is to map source relationships broadly.
  • The Times moved to quash the subpoenas before US District Judge Arun Subramanian in Manhattan; press-freedom groups condemned the demand for relatives' records.

Left view

Truthout and the Freedom of the Press Foundation (cited by TheWrap) call the subpoenas an assault on press freedom, arguing that dragging in journalists' family members is designed to intimidate and to unmask confidential sources rather than investigate a specific leak.

Right view

Mainstream conservative coverage of this specific story is thin; the administration's general defense — echoed in prior leak fights — is that unauthorized disclosures of presidential-security details endanger the president and warrant aggressive investigation. Coverage here is led by center and left-leaning outlets.

Watch for

Subramanian's ruling on the motion to quash will set a marker for how far DOJ can reach into reporters' and third parties' records. A loss for the Times could chill national-security sourcing; a win could reinstate stronger media-shield norms. Expect appeals either way.

Federal judge freezes Paramount Skydance's $110B Warner Bros. deal

Facts

  • US District Judge Araceli Martínez Olguín issued a 14-day temporary restraining order blocking Paramount Skydance from closing its $110 billion takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery.
  • The judge said the merger "likely" violates antitrust law; the companies had hoped to close as soon as July 22.
  • Twelve state attorneys general, led by California's Rob Bonta, sued July 13, arguing the deal would combine two of Hollywood's five remaining major studios and violate Section 7 of the Clayton Act.
  • A preliminary-injunction hearing is set for Aug. 3. Paramount (PSKY) shares fell toward record lows.

Left view

The Washington Post and the Democratic-led state coalition frame the pause as a needed antitrust check on media consolidation that would shrink competition, raise prices and reduce choice for consumers and creators alike.

Right view

Business-oriented coverage (Bloomberg, Axios) emphasizes deal certainty and shareholder value, noting the freeze could unravel a painstakingly negotiated transaction and warning that state AGs stacking antitrust suits injects political risk into M&A.

Watch for

The Aug. 3 hearing decides whether the deal stalls for months. A preliminary injunction would pressure Paramount's financing and could reopen talks on divestitures; a win for the companies clears a fast close. Either outcome signals how aggressively courts will police media mergers.
