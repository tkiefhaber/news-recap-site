The Big Stories Today

Duffy now blames contractor L3Harris for the ground stop as Saturday cancellations top 6,400

Facts

  • Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said Saturday the outage began during L3Harris's routine work moving an FAA telecom line from copper to fiber at a remote site. A router there flooded the network, and the problem spread to routers at other air traffic sites. Investigators haven't settled on operator error, a bad installation or a faulty router (Fox Business, CNBC).
  • L3Harris, which has run the FAA's telecom network since 2002, said only that it is "supporting the FAA in its investigation" and that its copper-to-fiber conversion is more than 70% done. Officials again ruled out a cyberattack, and FAA chief Bryan Bedford said there was "zero evidence of anything AI related."
  • FlightAware counted more than 6,400 U.S. cancellations Saturday, above the FAA's forecast of 4,000–5,000. About 800 Sunday flights, mostly American and JetBlue, were already scrubbed. The FAA expects schedules to be back to normal by the end of today (Yahoo News).

Left view

Democrats are going after Duffy personally. New York Gov. Kathy Hochul called for him to resign, and Sen. Tammy Duckworth faulted him for blaming Congress a day earlier and now blaming a contractor (Yahoo News). NPR's account leads with the FAA's own description of an "equipment issue" during a network upgrade, so the cause sounds more like a routine maintenance job gone wrong than decades of neglect.

Right view

Fox Business framed the story around Duffy's account. It quoted his line that he is "stuck with L3Harris" until a new system is built and connected that to his $50B overhaul request with AT&T, Verizon and Lumen. The White House called Hochul's demand a political stunt. Not every Republican is defending leadership, though: GOP Rep. Tim Burchett criticized his own party's leaders over aviation funding (The Hill).

Watch for

Bipartisan reviews are coming: the House Transportation Committee's Sam Graves and Rick Larsen, plus Senate Commerce's Maria Cantwell. Watch for L3Harris (LHX) shares to react Monday, and for whether the FAA pauses daytime network maintenance before the Thanksgiving rush. NATCA's Nick Daniels said "blame changes nothing," and Bedford warned the network "will continue to break until we replace it."

Hormuz flares again: Iran says tankers hit mines and it downed a U.S. drone; CENTCOM rejects Bahrain strike claim

Facts

  • Iran's navy says two oil tankers caught fire Saturday after hitting mines south of the Strait of Hormuz. It says both were using an unauthorized route with their transponders off, and calls it the fifth incident in 24 hours involving ships struck by Iran or by mines. None of this has been independently verified (ANI/Tribune, Anadolu).
  • The IRGC says it shot down a U.S. MQ-1 drone over Qeshm Island, which it calls the 17th, and claims it destroyed a U.S. early-warning radar in Bahrain. CENTCOM says U.S. forces shot down both Iranian one-way attack drones aimed at Bahrain, with no damage or injuries.
  • The U.S. says the main routes are cleared of mines and its blockade of Iranian ports remains in place. Trump says "we don't need a deal with Iran." The IAEA says it is "increasingly concerned" about having no access to buried enriched uranium at Isfahan (CBS News).

Left view

Mainstream and left-of-center coverage (CBS News live blog, AP) focuses on the risk of a war with no exit: Trump says the war is "militarily effectively over" while tankers burn and inspectors are locked out. Commentary from the left was thin overnight. Most outlets ran wire copy.

Right view

Fox News' coverage has stressed the IMO's warning that even the internationally recognized shipping lane isn't certified mine-free. It has also highlighted Tehran's use of a Qatar-brokered seven-point offer to pressure Washington, with CENTCOM's rebuttals given prominence. Right-leaning commentary beyond Fox was thin overnight, so this story is being led by wire and state-media claims.

Watch for

Brent is near $96, and the IEA says global supply now exceeds demand for the first time since the war began. That cushions prices unless more ships are hit. Iran's chief negotiator says U.S. officials asked Putin to help broker talks, which puts this week's Shenzhen summit (below) in play. A confirmed strike on U.S. forces in Bahrain would sharply raise the odds of renewed U.S. strikes.

Trump–Xi–Putin summit set for Thursday in Shenzhen; Zelensky says U.S. floated Donbas-for-NATO swap

Facts

  • The Kremlin and White House confirmed the first-ever trilateral meeting of the three leaders on Nov. 19, on the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen. Expected topics are nuclear arms control (New START expired in February), Iran and Hormuz, and energy. A senior U.S. official said: "We do not expect to get any kind of agreement" (TBS/AFP, TASS).
  • Ukraine isn't formally on the agenda. Envoys Witkoff and Kushner are running a separate peace track, though the Kremlin says Putin could raise it.
  • Zelensky said U.S. negotiators, in talks in Abu Dhabi and Ireland, asked whether Ukraine would cede parts of Donbas that Russia doesn't hold in exchange for NATO membership. He called it the first time NATO membership had been tied to territory. He refuses to cede land, rejects neutrality, and would put any deal to a referendum only with a full package of security guarantees and money (Al Jazeera, CNBC). Neither the White House nor the Kremlin has commented.

Left view

The Guardian's headline puts it bluntly: the U.S. "proposes Kyiv cede land to Russia for Nato membership," with the U.S. cast as the proposer and Moscow as the winner. Reuters' analysis, "A new 'Yalta'?", says Trump and Putin are pushing for the summit while Xi is wary of hosting a show that could look like great powers carving up spheres.

Right view

The hawkish right is alarmed. In The Dispatch, Leon Aron compares the summit to Munich 1938 and calls it "a gift to an expansionist dictator." The Washington Times reports the White House confirmation straight, without commentary. The National's Sholto Byrnes, from outside the U.S., argues the Yalta fears are overblown and the meeting will be mostly symbolic.

Watch for

Lancaster University's Simon Mabon expects something "more performative" than substantive. The concrete things to watch are any arms-control framework that includes China, any movement on Hormuz (China depends on Gulf oil), and whether Megaspeed/Nvidia export curbs and rare earths come up. In Europe, the UK, France, Germany, Italy and Poland formed a negotiating bloc for talks with the U.S. and Kyiv, a sign they are worried about being left out.

U.S. loses measles-elimination status for the first time since 2000

Facts

  • The Pan American Health Organization has concluded the U.S. no longer meets the bar of no continuous local spread for 12+ months, after a Utah outbreak passed that mark in June. The finding was first reported by the WSJ and confirmed by KFF Health News and CBS. PAHO plans a formal announcement around Nov. 17 (Washington Post, KFF).
  • The CDC has tallied 5,412 cases this year, more than double 2025's total, across 45 states and D.C. There have been 590+ hospitalizations and at least six deaths by CDC count (states report more). Pennsylvania's outbreak alone tops 1,500 cases. About 96% of cases were in unvaccinated people or people with unknown status (CBS News).
  • HHS spokesperson Emily Hilliard said vaccination gaps predate this administration, denied suppressing data and cited about $100M in extra outbreak funding. The White House says Trump and Kennedy have repeatedly urged measles shots (KFF).

Left view

The left blames Kennedy directly. HuffPost reports he is being "shamed" online, and KFF Health News details CDC officials' accounts that political appointees delayed or blocked outbreak reports. KFF also lists cuts to state immunization grants, the remaking of the CDC vaccine advisory panel, and Trump's false claim that MMR can be "quite lethal." Truthout ran the news under the line that the U.S. has "officially" lost the status.

Right view

Right-leaning coverage mostly reported the facts without assigning blame. Townhall focused on the 92.4% school MMR rate and the MMR shot's ~97% effectiveness, with no criticism of HHS. The administration's defense, echoed in conservative coverage, is that the decline began in the pandemic years. Fox 5 NY led with the WSJ scoop and an explainer on what the status loss means.

Watch for

Regaining status means ending every current chain of spread and stopping new outbreaks within a year. CDC's Paul Rota says that is "not on the horizon." Expect more state fights over non-medical exemptions; states that ended them saw kindergarten vaccination rates rise 2–4 points (CBS's Céline Gounder). Also expect the U.S. to be treated as a measles exporter. The West Texas outbreak already spread to Mexico.

Midterm stragglers break blue: Boebert ousted in Colorado, Peltola declares victory in Alaska

Facts

  • CNN projects Democrat Eileen Laubacher, a retired Navy rear admiral, has unseated Rep. Lauren Boebert in Colorado's 4th District, 48.8% to 47.9% with about 98% counted (Newsweek). Trump endorsed Boebert in late October after calling for a primary challenge against her over the summer (Colorado Politics).
  • In Alaska, Mary Peltola has widened her lead over Sen. Dan Sullivan with about 96% counted and declared victory Saturday. She is still just under 50%, so ranked-choice tabulation runs Nov. 18, and certification is Nov. 25 (TBS/AFP, Washington Times).
  • It's the last uncalled Senate race. A Peltola win would give Democrats a 56-seat majority, versus 55 if Sullivan holds on.

Left view

MS NOW now rates Alaska "likely Democratic" and treats it as the capstone of the wave. The Advocate and AlterNet celebrated Boebert's defeat as the fall of a MAGA figurehead. Democracy Docket says Peltola now leads both Sullivans on the ballot combined, which would make the ranked-choice count a formality.

Right view

Conservative outlets are mostly reporting the results rather than spinning them. The Washington Times ran Peltola's victory claim straight, and Sullivan has not conceded. Colorado Politics, which covered the race closely, has noted Trump's on-again, off-again relationship with Boebert. Right-leaning outlets have mostly not defended her campaign.

Watch for

The Nov. 18 ranked-choice count in Alaska. A 56-seat Senate would give Democrats more room in December's Schumer–Van Hollen leadership fight and on confirmations. Remaining House calls will set the size of the new Democratic majority.
