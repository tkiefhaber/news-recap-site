Federal judge blocks Trump's mail-in voting order nationwide

Facts

  • US District Judge Indira Talwani (Boston) issued a nationwide order Aug. 11 barring the Postal Service from carrying out Trump's directives to overhaul mail-in voting before the November midterms, expanding an earlier partial block.
  • Trump's March order asked USPS to build a system regulating who could vote by mail and let it refuse to deliver ballots in non-cooperating states, citing alleged noncitizen voting.
  • Talwani ruled the president "has no authority to regulate elections"; it is the second legal hold on the order, with the first pending before the Supreme Court's 6–3 conservative majority.

Left view

NPR and Votebeat frame the ruling as a check on executive overreach, stressing the judge's finding that election administration belongs to states and Congress, not the White House, and warning the order risked disenfranchising voters.

Right view

Fox News coverage centers the administration's stated goal of election integrity and preventing noncitizen voting, and notes the fight is not over — a favorable Supreme Court ruling could revive the order's core provisions.

Watch for

All eyes on the Supreme Court, which could rule before ballots are printed. The outcome shapes mail-voting mechanics in swing states for a midterm that will decide control of Congress.
