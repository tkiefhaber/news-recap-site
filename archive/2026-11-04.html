The Big Stories Today

Democrats sweep Congress, flipping both the Senate and the House

Facts

  • AP called the Senate for Democrats Tuesday night and the House early Wednesday. Hakeem Jeffries is now in line to become the first Black House speaker.
  • Senate flips include Ohio (Sherrod Brown over Sen. Jon Husted), Kansas (Adam Hamilton over Sen. Roger Marshall), Iowa (Josh Turek over Ashley Hinson), North Carolina (Roy Cooper), South Carolina (Annie Andrews over Darline Graham) and Texas, where James Talarico beat Ken Paxton to end the GOP's 32-year statewide winning streak there. Forbes reports Troy Jackson unseated Sen. Susan Collins in Maine. Democrats held Georgia (Ossoff), New Hampshire (Pappas) and Minnesota (Flanagan).
  • Republicans held Florida's Senate seat (Ashley Moody, about 51–48) and the governorship (Byron Donalds), plus Wyoming (Harriet Hageman). Some House races and ranked-choice counts in Maine and Alaska are still being tallied. Cook Political Report had projected Democratic House gains of up to about 25 seats.
  • Exit polls: about 60% of voters disapproved of Trump's job performance, and about 90% called cost of living a major factor. Gas averaged $4.15 on Election Day, the first Election Day above $4 (AAA, via Forbes).

Left view

The Guardian calls it a "blue wave" that hands Democrats real power to check Trump's agenda. MS NOW and Daily Kos treat the Texas and Kansas wins as signs the map has been redrawn, not just a normal midterm swing. AP's takeaways frame it as voters rebuking two years of a compliant GOP Congress. Democrats are already signaling oversight: likely Oversight chair Rep. Robert Garcia told Fox News Digital, "Lawyer up."

Right view

Fox News called it an "unprecedented blue wave," pointing to GOP losses with Hispanic, young, independent and rural voters, and led with Democrats' investigation threats. Ben Shapiro called it a "wipeout" and said the president was effectively on the ballot. The pro-Trump NY Post ran a "blue tsunami" front page that pinned the losses on him, and the WSJ editorial board called for a GOP reckoning. In The Free Press, editors asked whether this was a referendum on Trump or the usual second-term penalty.

Watch for

AP and PBS expect gridlock: Trump has vowed to veto Democratic priorities, and a Democratic Senate can now block his judicial and Supreme Court picks. Expect House subpoenas on the Iran war, DHS and administration ethics starting in January. Stalled spending fights and the debt limit move to the top of the lame-duck agenda. Analysts will also watch whether Democrats can hold voters who turned out against Trump rather than for the party (Free Press).

Trump says the losses aren't on him as the GOP blame game starts

Facts

  • In a phone interview with Fox News, Trump said he doesn't see the results as a hit on him, noted he "wasn't on the ballot," and said Republicans "just didn't inspire" voters. On Truth Social he said he would have won in a landslide.
  • CNN reports Trump blamed his own handpicked candidates. The NYT reports other Republicans put the blame squarely on him. A CBS headline says he acknowledged the Iran war played a part.
  • Going in, his approval sat near 38% in Nate Silver's tracker. Before the vote he floated a preemptive pardon for Elon Musk if Democrats won.

Left view

PBS says the results leave Trump with sharply reduced political capital at home and abroad. Rolling Stone and HuffPost focus on the deflection, saying a president who campaigned in nearly every battleground can't separate himself from the result. The common argument on the left, echoed in AP exit-poll coverage, is that the backlash was about prices, the war and corruption, not candidate quality.

Right view

The right is split. Loyalists on Fox emphasize Trump's point that he wasn't on the ballot and blame weak candidates and turnout, noting Georgia's GOP vote fell short of strategists' targets. The Murdoch papers and conservative commentators (NY Post, WSJ editorial board, Shapiro) argue the war, gas prices and tariffs were Trump's choices and that voters judged him. Caitlin Flanagan wrote in The Free Press that kitchen-table costs drove the anger.

Watch for

Lame-duck dynamics: watch whether GOP senators facing 2028 races start breaking with the White House on Iran war powers and tariffs. Leadership fights in the House GOP conference are likely. Also watch for aggressive executive actions and pardons before the new Congress is sworn in on Jan. 3.

US strikes Iran's Qeshm Island as Hormuz talks stall; post-election strike plans in focus

Facts

  • US forces hit an Iranian anti-aircraft site on Qeshm Island, near the Strait of Hormuz, early this week. Officials called the strikes "defensive," a response to Iranian fire at a US aircraft. Trump called it "minor" and said it "wasn't war."
  • Iran's Mohsen Rezaei said the US reply to Tehran's seven-condition plan to reopen Hormuz was "not convincing." The conditions include lifting sanctions and ending the US naval blockade. Qatar says it is relaying proposals in real time and working toward an interim deal (Al Jazeera, CBS/Reuters).
  • US media reported CENTCOM had plans for strikes that could start as soon as today, with three carriers arriving in the region. Bloomberg reports Trump is now playing down that prospect after the midterm rout.
  • Shipping attacks continue: UKMTO reported a tanker hit by two projectiles off Oman, and a dhow attack in the strait killed one seafarer.

Left view

NPR and the Guardian frame the war, now in its ninth month, as a central driver of the GOP's losses, and Democrats are expected to push war-powers votes as soon as they take control. Critics such as John Mearsheimer, quoted by Al Jazeera, warn that another short bombing campaign won't achieve US goals and could pull the US into a longer war.

Right view

Fox News emphasizes the strikes' defensive nature and Trump's claim that Iran's forces have been "decapitated." AP reports Netanyahu is pressing Trump for a decisive post-election campaign. Trump told the FT the US is "getting everything we want," and he has also said US aid to Israel will end by year-end.

Watch for

Qatar expected feedback from both sides within about 36 hours. A deal to reopen Hormuz would likely knock oil sharply lower. Renewed large-scale strikes would push Brent back toward the $115+ levels seen in late October. Iran has hinted at hitting Gulf undersea cables or European targets if attacked.

California voters appear to reject the nation's first billionaire wealth tax

Facts

  • With about half the expected vote counted, Prop. 40, a one-time 5% tax on the net worth of the state's roughly 200 billionaires, was losing about 44–56 (San José Inside). The union sponsor had projected about $100 billion for healthcare and education over five years.
  • Rival Prop. 42, which bans wealth taxes, was leading about 55–45. If it finishes with more yes votes than Prop. 40, it overrides the tax under the state constitution. Prop. 41 was trailing.
  • Opponents spent about $317 million, including $102 million from Sergey Brin. Gov. Newsom backed Prop. 42, while the state Democratic Party and labor federation backed Prop. 40.
  • Elsewhere, local measures on data centers were on ballots in Ohio, Illinois, Wisconsin and Florida. Ohio alone had 21, including outright bans in 13 municipalities (Forbes).

Left view

Progressive backers and the California Budget & Policy Center argue the state faces deep health-care cuts and that the measure was buried by record billionaire spending. Some on the left note that a Democratic wave still didn't carry a wealth tax, even in California.

Right view

Business groups and conservative outlets read the result as proof that even blue-state voters reject wealth taxes, warning that the threat alone was pushing founders and capital out of California. They see Newsom's support for Prop. 42 as confirmation. Coverage so far comes mostly from California outlets; national right-leaning coverage is thin.

Watch for

California counts slowly, so final margins could take weeks. Expect legal fights over how competing measures interact if margins tighten. The data-center votes are an early test of local backlash against AI infrastructure, a theme Amundi's Aidan Yao flagged as a market risk.

Watch for
