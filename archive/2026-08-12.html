July inflation report lands as markets watch for a Fed rate cut

Facts

  • The Bureau of Labor Statistics released the July Consumer Price Index at 8:30 a.m. ET today.
  • Economists surveyed by Dow Jones expected headline inflation of 3.4% year-over-year, down from 3.5% in June.
  • Core CPI (excluding food and energy) was projected to rise 0.2% on the month and ease to 2.5% annually.
  • Average hourly wage growth of about 3.2% is still running below headline inflation.

Left view

Outlets like NPR and the Washington Post frame stubborn 3%-plus inflation and wages lagging prices as evidence that tariffs and trade disruptions are quietly taxing households, arguing the Fed should be cautious about declaring victory.

Right view

The WSJ editorial page and CNBC-cited analysts emphasize the cooling trend and easing energy costs as a sign disinflation is on track, pressing the Fed to move toward rate cuts to support growth rather than keeping policy tight.

Watch for

Economists note the print heavily shapes odds of a September Fed cut; a soft reading could lift equities and pull Treasury yields lower, while an upside surprise driven by energy would revive tariff-and-oil inflation worries.

Trump signs order to shrink the childhood vaccine schedule

Facts

  • President Trump signed an executive order Monday calling to reduce the number of recommended childhood shots to 11.
  • The order recommends splitting the combined MMR (measles, mumps, rubella) vaccine into three separate shots.
  • It directs federal agencies to study the schedule and report back within 90 days; it makes no immediate changes to vaccine access.
  • Trump referenced autism repeatedly at the signing, though the order text does not address it; the vaccine-autism link has been repeatedly debunked.

Left view

ABC News, the Washington Post and NPR highlight sharp pushback from pediatricians and public-health groups, stressing decades of studies backing the current schedule and warning that splitting MMR into separate shots is untested and could lower coverage.

Right view

The White House frames it as "gold standard" recommendations restoring parental choice and scientific scrutiny; supportive commentators cast it as accountability for a public-health establishment they say lost public trust during the pandemic.

Watch for

The real fight comes when agency reports land in ~90 days; medical societies may keep issuing their own guidance, and insurers, states and school-mandate rules could diverge from any revised federal schedule.

Colombia digs out from its strongest earthquake in years

Facts

  • A magnitude 7.4 earthquake struck western Colombia on Aug. 10, with the epicenter near San José del Palmar, about 150 miles west of Bogotá.
  • The confirmed death toll has climbed past 200 (reports around 224), with thousands injured and many still missing.
  • Colombia's Geological Service has logged more than 70 aftershocks; the government declared a national disaster and deployed the military.
  • Collapsed structures included pediatric and neonatal wards of a hospital in Cali; rescuers pulled survivors from rubble more than a day later.

Left view

NPR and Al Jazeera coverage emphasizes underinvestment in an impoverished region, building-code gaps and the need for robust international humanitarian aid to reach isolated communities near the epicenter.

Right view

With little explicitly partisan framing, right-leaning coverage is thin; outlets like Time and NBC focus on the government's disaster response, logistics of the rescue effort and the scale of the rebuild ahead.

Watch for

Aid agencies warn the toll will likely keep rising as crews reach the epicenter; watch for disease and shelter risks among the displaced and pressure on President Petro's government over relief speed.

Senate crypto "market structure" bill inches toward a floor vote

Facts

  • On Aug. 8, the Senate acted to advance the CLARITY Act crypto market-structure bill before leaving for August recess.
  • Majority Leader John Thune set up a key procedural vote for when the Senate returns in mid-September, teeing up a possible full floor vote.
  • The bill merges Senate Banking and Agriculture Committee frameworks and includes a ban on presidents and federal officials issuing or sponsoring crypto.
  • Passage needs all voting Republicans plus at least eight Democrats; ethics provisions remain the sticking point.

Left view

Democrats including Sens. Alsobrooks and Gallego, per CNBC and Roll Call, insist on tougher conduct rules — pointing at the president's own crypto ventures — and warn light-touch regulation risks another investor blowup.

Right view

Republicans and industry-aligned outlets frame the bill as overdue regulatory "clarity" that keeps crypto innovation and jobs in the US, arguing endless delay drives the sector offshore.

Watch for

The September procedural vote is the real test; a deal on ethics language could unlock bipartisan passage and boost digital-asset markets, while a stall keeps the sector in regulatory limbo.

DR Congo Ebola outbreak becomes the country's largest on record

Facts

  • Congolese officials report more than 2,000 deaths out of roughly 4,381 confirmed cases — the DRC's largest Ebola outbreak ever and one of the fastest-growing.
  • It is caused by the Bundibugyo strain, for which there is no approved vaccine or treatment.
  • The WHO says the outbreak is intensifying at an "exceptional" pace and has urged a major scale-up of response.
  • The US State Department announced an additional $242 million for Ebola response in the DRC and Uganda.

Left view

Guardian and UN-cited coverage stresses global-health solidarity and warns that cuts or gaps in international funding leave outbreaks to spread, urging robust aid and support for overstretched local health workers.

Right view

Right-leaning coverage is thin here; where present, the emphasis is on accountability for how aid dollars are spent and on border and travel screening to keep the outbreak contained rather than open-ended commitments.

Watch for

Health officials warn the toll could keep climbing without a vaccine for this strain; watch for cross-border spread into Uganda and strain on the response as new treatment centers open.
