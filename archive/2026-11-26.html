The Big Stories Today

USTR formally proposes Section 301 tariffs on China, the EU and a dozen-plus partners; earliest start is January

Facts

  • On Thanksgiving eve, US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer proposed new tariffs after two Section 301 probes into "excess capacity" and unfair trade practices. Section 301 is the main route left after the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs in February.
  • Proposed rates per TIME: China up to 20% on most covered goods, rising to 40% if Beijing breaks last year's truce. The EU gets 15% all-in, rising to 20% if deals aren't honored. Japan and South Korea face up to 15%, Vietnam up to 35%, and India, Taiwan, Switzerland and several Southeast Asian economies 15% to 25%+. Mexico and Norway are still pending, and Brazil faces a separate proposal aimed at sugar and aircraft.
  • Comments are due Jan. 5, and nothing takes effect before January 2027. Greer told CNBC much of it is already "baked in."
  • China's Foreign Ministry called "overcapacity" a pretext for political manipulation. The European Commission rejected the premise and said it expects the 15% ceiling from the 2025 deal to hold.

Left view

Left-leaning outlets (Raw Story, Democracy Now) cast the move as Trump ignoring the midterm verdict. They lean on the NBC exit poll, cited by TIME, in which 66% of voters said tariffs hurt the economy. Georgetown's Kathleen Claussen told TIME the plan may mostly be leverage to push partners into acting against China.

Right view

The right is split. The WSJ editorial board ("Post-Election Message: More Tariffs") panned it as anti-growth. It argued the exemptions for coffee and other staples concede that tariffs raise prices, and warned that the "subsidy" rationale could be turned on US chip subsidies. Greer, in a recent letter to the WSJ, countered that foreign exporters absorbed 40–50% of the 2025 tariffs and that 19 partners signed deals.

Watch for

Retaliation lists from Beijing and Brussels, and whether the plan disrupts the post-APEC truce ahead of December's G20 in Miami. Stocks of importers and retailers are exposed heading into holiday sales. The comment period ends Jan. 5, and Democrats take the House in January.

Hot October inflation all but locks in a December Fed hike as AI stocks whipsaw

Facts

  • The Fed's preferred gauge, PCE, rose 3.8% year over year in October, up from 3.6%. Core PCE was 3.3%, above the 3.2% forecast and up from 3.1%. Both rose 0.3% on the month (Commerce Dept., via Yahoo Finance).
  • Q3 GDP was revised up to 4.3% (Fox Business).
  • CME FedWatch puts the odds of a hike at the Dec. 9 meeting at about 97–98%, and Fox Business puts the odds of a second hike in January at around 63%. Governors Waller and Cook have argued for hikes. NY Fed President Williams ruled out an oversized move.
  • On Wednesday, a new AI and chip sell-off briefly put the Nasdaq-100 20% below its record. Nvidia closed down 3.9%. The Dow erased a steep morning loss to finish higher (Fox Business, Yahoo Finance).

Left view

Partisan framing is thin, and coverage is mostly in the financial press. Center-left economists stress the household pain. Navy Federal's Heather Long called it "another hot inflation reading," and outlets noted that housing is already weakening as mortgage rates jump (RISMedia).

Right view

Right-leaning business outlets point to strong growth. Fox Business led with the 4.3% GDP revision and the Dow's comeback. The WSJ editorial page tied price pressure partly to tariffs and warned that a Fed tightening into a wobbly market is the wrong moment for new import taxes.

Watch for

Capital Economics calls a December hike "certain," with January possibly needed too. Truist's Mike Skordeles is calmer, noting easy year-ago comparisons. Expect higher mortgage and credit-card rates into 2027 and pressure on long-duration tech valuations. The VIX is still elevated near 27.

Ukraine hits two Samara refineries and a bomber base as Putin vows retaliation and US Patriot deliveries stall

Facts

  • Ukraine's General Staff confirmed overnight strikes on Rosneft's Kuibyshev and Syzran refineries in Samara, about 800 km from the border. Together they handle roughly 6% of Russia's crude throughput (Kyiv Independent, ISW).
  • Kyiv also says it damaged three MiG-31s and two Su-34s at Savasleyka air base. ISW counts 15 Russian refineries hit so far in November, and Deputy PM Novak has acknowledged strain on fuel supplies.
  • Putin called retaliation "inevitable." Russia launched 112 drones overnight, and Russian strikes killed at least seven people across six Ukrainian regions (HNGN, ISW).
  • Zelensky says at least 48 Patriot interceptors are waiting on Defense Secretary Hegseth's sign-off. US negotiators have floated Article 5-style guarantees, but Moscow still demands Ukraine leave Donbas first (Bloomberg via FDD, ISW).

Left view

Left-leaning outlets (Democracy Now, Daily Kos) highlight the Patriot hold-up as Washington leaning on Kyiv. They also note Zelensky's comment that he would turn to the new Democratic Congress if Trump can't get a deal by year-end.

Right view

Hawkish and right-of-center analysts (FDD, ISW) frame the deep strikes as Ukraine building leverage by hitting Russia's oil revenue. They warn that delaying air defenses just as Putin promises retaliation invites a bigger Russian barrage. Mainstream right outlets gave the story less play than tariffs and immigration.

Watch for

A large Russian missile and drone strike on Ukraine's power grid as winter sets in. Russian gasoline export curbs could tighten diesel and product markets. Watch too for whether Patriots ship before year-end and how NATO answers Poland's call for a border no-fly zone.

Kilmar Ábrego García leaves the US for Spain, ending his deportation fight

Facts

  • Ábrego García flew commercially Tuesday and landed in Madrid early Wednesday with his wife, his lawyer and representatives of CASA (CBS News).
  • DHS said he left under a removal order and "will NEVER be allowed back." Judge Paula Xinis closed the case Wednesday and dissolved her injunctions.
  • The Tennessee smuggling charges were dismissed in May as vindictive. DOJ's appeal will be argued in the 6th Circuit in February. Fox, citing the NYT, says he may move on to Mexico.

Left view

NPR and NBC framed it as a man arriving "free" after a wrongful 2025 deportation to El Salvador and a long legal battle. His lawyer noted he flew United rather than an ICE plane, a point about leaving on his own terms.

Right view

Fox News led with administration officials. DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin said he was "delighted," and Karoline Leavitt said the White House was "vindicated," repeating the MS-13 allegations his lawyers dispute.

Watch for

The DOJ appeal in February could still matter for precedent on vindictive prosecution. Both sides will cite the case in coming fights over third-country removals.
