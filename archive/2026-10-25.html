US weighs new Iran strikes as Brent settles at $116.72, a five-month high

Facts

  • Brent crude settled at $116.72 on Friday (+3.1%), its highest close since April; WTI settled at $103.27. Brent was under $97 at the start of October (CBS News).
  • US Ambassador to Turkey Tom Barrack said a strike on Iran could come this weekend; Trump has said any strikes would wait until after the Nov. 3 midterms, while also saying the US "may do another little Epic Fury" (CBS, Fox News).
  • Iran's armed forces chief said US warships will not be allowed in the Persian Gulf. Iran says it is open to talks under the June Pakistan-brokered interim deal but blames the continuing US naval blockade for the stall (CBS).
  • The Director of National Intelligence warned in a memo that Iran has stepped up efforts to target US personnel abroad; the State Department updated advisories for 14 countries and ordered non-essential staff out of Saudi Arabia.
  • Two supertankers reportedly hit mines on an unauthorized southern Hormuz route Friday, per the IRGC; Reuters reports Tehran has drawn up plans to damage Gulf undersea internet cables if strikes resume.

Left view

Left-leaning and international outlets (The Guardian, CBS live coverage, Slate) tie the escalation to household costs and the midterms, noting that Trump's own timeline puts action after Election Day and that UN Ambassador Mike Waltz has said the war could last another year.

Right view

Fox News emphasizes the pressure campaign: Treasury Secretary Bessent says Iranian oil revenue is being choked off, Kpler projects it could hit zero by mid-December, and Netanyahu says Trump told him the US is "ready to finish the job." Trump accused Iran of faking ship strikes to push up oil prices.

Watch for

Analysts quoted across outlets see crude staying elevated while Hormuz and Saudi infrastructure remain at risk; a strike before Nov. 3 would contradict Trump's stated timeline and could send oil sharply higher. Putin is due to host Iran's president in Moscow Oct. 27.
