Trump rejects Iran's 7-day Hormuz plan, but expects talks to resume this week

Facts

  • Iran Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi offered a plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days and restart nuclear talks, conditioned on sanctions relief and a regional ceasefire; Trump rejected it, saying it is not the deal he wants and resembles what might have been acceptable a year ago.
  • Trump says he expects more talks with Iran this week, with Qatari mediators reportedly facilitating contacts in New York, and would not rule out more strikes before the midterms (“It's possible, but I just don't want to say that”).
  • Iranian President Pezeshkian told CBS his country would allow UN nuclear inspectors as part of a ceasefire deal. Treasury Secretary Bessent predicts Iran will run out of oil deliveries within about two weeks.
  • Markets reacted: WTI rose about 4.2% to $96.31 and Brent about 4.0% to $108.50 on Monday, per TheStreet.

Left view

NPR and CBS coverage highlights Rep. Jim Himes (D-Conn.) arguing the military approach has not worked and that Iran, through oil prices, effectively has leverage over what happens on Election Day. Other left-leaning coverage stresses the standoff is raising costs for consumers.

Right view

Fox News frames the rejection as strength, citing Republican officials who say Iran is miscalculating if it expects midterm pressure to soften Trump. Sen. John Kennedy (R-La.) called the offer “not a serious offer” and backed continued sanctions and blockade.

Watch for

Analysts cited by market outlets tie oil directly to whether talks restart. If they stall, expect crude to stay in the $100+ Brent range and gasoline to keep climbing into November; a credible reopening of the strait would likely pull prices sharply lower. Bessent's two-week oil-exhaustion claim sets an informal clock.

FAA delays Boeing 737 MAX 10 certification over software issue; stock falls about 6-7%

Facts

  • The FAA said it will delay certifying the 737 MAX 10 after finding a software problem, supplied by GE Aerospace, that could prevent pilots accessing automated flight guidance in certain go-around scenarios.
  • FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford said the agency will wait until it is satisfied there is no issue, and will convene a Corrective Action Review Board; he would not say whether the fix takes days, weeks or months.
  • Boeing shares fell roughly 6-7% Monday (TheStreet: 6.2%; CP24: 6.9%). WestJet said it saw no in-service issues and did not view it as a safety concern.

Left view

Left-leaning coverage in what I found is limited; where present it links the delay to broader questions about Boeing's safety culture and regulatory rigor after prior MAX problems.

Right view

Business-oriented and right-leaning coverage focuses on the financial hit and competitive risk versus Airbus, with some emphasis that the FAA is being cautious rather than facing a proven in-service failure.

Watch for

Analysts see a further delay to Boeing's single-aisle competitiveness against Airbus and possible pressure on delivery and cash-flow targets. The review board's timeline is the key variable.
