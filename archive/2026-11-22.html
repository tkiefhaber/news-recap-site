The Big Stories Today

Russian missiles land in Poland; Trump calls them "little" and accidental, and Tusk pushes back hard

Facts

  • Two Russian missiles, including a Kh-101 cruise missile, and a suspected jet-powered drone came down in southeast Poland on Friday during Russia's overnight barrage on Ukraine. No injuries or damage were reported in Poland (AP via PBS, Al Jazeera).
  • The sites were roughly 30–50 km from the Ukrainian border. One missile landed about 35 km from where a European exercise, "Koalicja 26.2," is rehearsing a possible postwar Ukraine mission (AP, Al Jazeera).
  • NATO chief Mark Rutte called it a "grave violation" of Polish airspace and is expected in Poland this weekend. Poland has not invoked Article 4, which it did in September 2025 (PBS, Al Jazeera).
  • In a Fox News phone interview, Trump said Putin had been landing drones and "a couple of little missiles" in Poland "by mistake." Tusk replied on X, "God protect us from such friends," and said nobody will pretend anymore this was an innocent accident (UPI).
  • Poland's defense minister called it "not accidental." Russia hasn't claimed it (UPI, Daily Beast).

Left view

The Daily Beast says Trump was "humiliated." It argues his defense of Putin backfired within hours and reports, citing Bloomberg, that European leaders received his message "very cautiously." Al Jazeera's explainer reads the strike as deliberate Russian probing of NATO and is skeptical of the U.S.-led peace track.

Right view

Right-leaning outlets have mostly run wire copy. Breitbart republished the UPI story without comment, and Fox's role was mainly hosting the Trump interview. The administration's case is that keeping channels to Putin open is what makes a deal possible. Rubio said Friday it would be "dangerous" for the leaders not to talk. Mainstream and left coverage is far heavier than the right's on this story.

Watch for

Analysts quoted by Al Jazeera (Scott Lucas, UCD; Harry Nedelcu, Rasmussen Global) expect Russia to keep testing the alliance. They see a defensive NATO response, such as more air defenses under Eastern Sentry, as more likely than an Article 5 escalation. Britain's defence intelligence chief told AP his biggest fear is "a significant miscalculation." A visible U.S.–Poland split could weaken Washington's hand in the Ukraine talks.

Tankers hit in Hormuz and Bahrain says Iran struck an industrial site, as Qatar quietly mediates

Facts

  • UKMTO reported two tankers hit Saturday off Khasab, Oman. One was struck at the stern and one in the engine room by an "unknown projectile." No casualties were reported. Iran's navy said a tanker ignoring warnings caught fire after hitting a mine in a restricted zone (AFP/Reuters via Al Jazeera).
  • Bahrain says Iran targeted a "civilian industrial facility," causing a fire. Residents saw smoke over Sitra Island, home to a Bapco refinery. The IRGC claimed missile and drone strikes on U.S. positions in Bahrain and said it hit two Patriot radars (AFP via TheJournal.ie).
  • Citing Iran's armed forces, Al Jazeera counts about 25 shipping incidents in a week, roughly three a day. Iran says ships must follow its lanes or risk attack or IRGC mines.
  • A Qatari delegation held two hours of talks in Tehran on Friday. FM Araghchi said the U.S. blockade must be lifted and that Iran's enriched uranium won't leave the country. Rolling blackouts begin in Iran today (AFP).

Left view

Al Jazeera frames it as Iran "tightening its grip" on the strait. Its Tehran correspondent called the rise in attacks "quite dramatic" and said it is becoming a pattern, which raises doubts that U.S. escorts and the blockade are working. Critics of the war in Congress keep losing war-powers votes. The latest failed 51-48.

Right view

Hawks point to results: CENTCOM says it has helped Gulf states export 1.5 billion barrels through Hormuz since summer and has cleared mines from the main routes (AFP). U.S. jets hit an Iranian missile site on Qeshm last week. Supporters of the "economic asphyxiation" blockade argue Iran's blackouts show the pressure is biting.

Watch for

Oil traders have shrugged so far. Brent fell for a third straight week, to about $92.57, as Gulf flows to Asia outweighed the strikes (EnergyNow, Archyde). A hit that actually disables Bapco or another Gulf refinery would change that. OPEC+ meets Nov. 29 on 2027 quotas. Also watch whether Qatar's channel produces a pause before Trump's stated post-New Year timeline.

Trump orders federal contractors to clear congressional requests with the White House before incoming Democrats start investigating

Facts

  • A late-Friday executive order tells all federal contractors and subcontractors to notify the White House of congressional information requests and to withhold material the administration deems privileged (Fox News, NPR).
  • Companies that don't comply risk losing contracts or being suspended from future work (Fox News).
  • The order frames it as protecting firms from lawmakers "harassing private companies." It follows a White House counsel directive requiring agency staff to clear congressional testimony first (Fox News).
  • Democrats won the House in the midterms. Rep. Robert Garcia, the expected Oversight chair, has already sent preservation letters to a dozen current and former officials, including Vance and Stephen Miller (Fox News, Al Jazeera).

Left view

MS NOW headlined it as an order meant to thwart Democrats' investigations. HuffPost and NPR led with the "check with the White House" requirement. Oversight Democrats say the probes, including ones into whether Trump's family profits from the presidency, are aimed at legislation and accountability, and they call the order an attempt to wall off the executive branch.

Right view

Fox News framed it in the order's own terms: a shield for private companies against partisan fishing expeditions, and a defense of executive privilege that every administration asserts. Supporters argue contractors shouldn't be a back door around privilege claims the White House would make directly.

Watch for

Fox notes legal challenges are likely, especially once House subpoenas start in January. Contractors such as defense primes and IT vendors are caught between a subpoena and the threat of suspension. Expect early test cases and a fight over whether privilege covers private firms at all.
