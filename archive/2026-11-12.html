The Big Stories Today

Nvidia board reportedly moves to oust Jensen Huang; stock sinks

Facts

  • The Wall Street Journal reported Wednesday evening that Nvidia's board is preparing to remove CEO Jensen Huang and could meet as soon as this week. The New York Times separately reported a second extramarital relationship, this one with a longtime Nvidia vice president.
  • Huang had already admitted an affair with a woman U.S. intelligence officials believe was a Chinese spy. The board opened an investigation but said it had no reason to believe national-security or company secrets were compromised.
  • Nvidia closed up 2.9% at $173.73 Wednesday, then fell about 9% after hours and was down roughly 5.5% premarket Thursday. Bloomberg names CFO Colette Kress and data-center chief Ian Buck as interim favorites. The company has no board chair and no public succession plan.
  • The White House says it isn't intervening, though it was reportedly aware of the plan. Trump has called Huang a friend and said the FBI is looking into the matter.

Left view

Democrats are focused on the administration's role. Sens. Elizabeth Warren and Chris Coons asked agencies to preserve records on Nvidia's dealings with China, often a first step toward an investigation. Outlets including NPR and The National Desk point to reports that Rubio briefed Trump on the spy concern in May, months after Trump loosened chip-export limits for Nvidia. Warren argues the company's access to Trump shaped those export decisions.

Right view

China hawks on the right treat this as a security failure. National Review called the scandal a "warning shot" about Beijing's reach into U.S. tech. Sen. Kevin Cramer (R-N.D.) called for Huang to resign, per The Hill, and Sen. Tom Cotton is pushing to blacklist Chinese memory maker CXMT. The Washington Examiner's coverage centers on the spy investigation rather than the White House.

Watch for

D.A. Davidson's Gil Luria says the scandal speeds up a leadership change Nvidia isn't ready for. The most valuable chip stock is already about 27% below its October record, so a messy handoff could weigh on the AI trade and the Nasdaq. Separately, Trump has reportedly authorized a crackdown on firms diverting Nvidia chips to China. That could tighten export rules no matter who runs the company.

Trump declares the Iran war won and seeks "total victory"; Tehran says no talks

Facts

  • Speaking at Arlington on Veterans Day, Trump said the roughly seven-month war is effectively over militarily and called for "total victory." He acknowledged 18 U.S. service members have died. He wants to keep the naval blockade of Iranian ports in place.
  • Iranian security chief Mohsen Rezaei said Tehran isn't negotiating, won't give up "a particle" of enriched uranium, and won't reopen the Strait of Hormuz until its seven conditions are met. The first is an end to fighting on all fronts, including Lebanon. He and FM Araghchi denied a WSJ report that he could meet VP Vance in Qatar.
  • The IRGC said tankers struck mines in Hormuz on Tuesday and Wednesday. Monitoring firms count at least 37 attacks on shipping in November, the highest monthly total of the war. Rubio said in Paris that economic pressure is working well enough that major combat isn't needed.

Left view

MS NOW opinion writers note the war is deeply unpopular. After their midterm wins, Democrats aren't waiting until January to try to cut off funding, and The Hill reports some Republicans are open to helping. Critics argue that a "won" war that still needs a blockade and destroyer escorts for tankers isn't over. They also note gas prices hurt Republicans at the polls.

Right view

Fox News led with Trump's case that Iran has been at war with the U.S. since 1979 and that the campaign is making the world safer. National Security Journal's Steve Balestrieri called the blockade-plus-pressure approach a reasonable strategy, possibly smarter than heavy bombing. He also warned the war is "probably" not close to ending. Reason argues from a libertarian angle that Congress should end it.

Watch for

Brent is holding just under $92. Iranian state TV commentators are urging a full Hormuz closure to push oil higher, so any break in mediation could lift crude again. Mediation runs through Pakistan, China, Iraq, Turkey and Qatar. Allianz Trade puts physical damage to Gulf states at more than $200 billion, with economic losses possibly reaching about four times that by 2030. Also watch whether a Vance–Rezaei channel survives Tehran's public denials.

Iran threat comes home: Bessent moves to a military base as DOJ details Fairford plot

Facts

  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, seen as the architect of Iran sanctions, has moved from his D.C. home onto a military base over death threats. He is the third Cabinet member living on a base, after Rubio and Hegseth.
  • A superseding indictment unsealed this week charges three men prosecutors tie to the IRGC and Kata'ib Hizballah. They are accused in nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks in Europe and the U.S., including a foiled September plot to drive fuel-laden vans at RAF Fairford in England. These are allegations, not findings of guilt.
  • Prosecutors say one defendant helped coordinate from a New York jail through coded calls to family in Iraq. The U.S. has pulled aircraft from RAF Mildenhall, its second drawdown from a British base in three months.

Left view

Left-leaning outlets have given this little coverage. Coverage is led by Fox News, RedState, PJ Media and Sinclair stations, with CNBC covering it from the wire/center. The main question from Democrats so far is broader: whether the war is making Americans less safe at home, which feeds their push to end it.

Right view

Fox News quoted an official saying the threats against Bessent have reached a "more than advanced stage." AG Todd Blanche said anyone plotting against U.S. troops will be prosecuted. Conservative outlets present the case as proof that Iran is an active terror threat and the war is justified.

Watch for

Security analysts expect tighter protection for officials and U.S. bases in Europe. Rezaei has threatened U.S. bases in Europe if strikes resume. Any attack on European soil would raise pressure on NATO allies already uneasy about hosting U.S. aircraft.

After Abu Dhabi, Witkoff and Kushner meet Putin in Moscow; Zelenskyy next in Dublin

Facts

  • Two days of U.S.-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi ended Wednesday without a breakthrough. They were the first direct Russia–Ukraine talks in nine months. The sides agreed to another major prisoner-of-war exchange, size not yet announced.
  • Envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner landed in Moscow Thursday for talks with Putin, Witkoff's 10th in-person meeting with him, per Reuters. They plan to see Zelenskyy Friday in Dublin, where he is attending a European Political Community summit.
  • Territory is still the main obstacle: Putin wants all of Donbas, and Kyiv refuses. U.S. mediators have shifted toward postwar security guarantees and reconstruction. Moscow wants Europe kept out of the talks, while Kyiv and Washington want Europe involved.

Left view

The Guardian led with Zelenskyy seeing a "window of opportunity," a cautiously hopeful framing. Along with CNN and France 24, it also reported that both sides struck each other's cities overnight during the diplomacy, a point many other outlets left out. Center-left coverage stresses Europe's exclusion as a risk to any lasting deal.

Right view

National Review argues the U.S. must reject Russia's narratives in the negotiations and shouldn't pressure Kyiv to cede land. Fox News framed the Moscow trip as peace talks "gaining momentum," in line with Trump's optimism.

Watch for

The POW swap is the likeliest concrete result in the near term. Analysts don't expect a ceasefire without movement on Donbas. Look for any Kremlin readout and Friday's Dublin meeting. Russia's floated non-aggression pacts with NATO states could resurface. European gas (Dutch TTF above €90/MWh) remains sensitive to Russia headlines.

Israel's new Knesset sworn in with no government as Eisenkot hunts for 61 seats

Facts

  • President Herzog gave Gadi Eisenkot the mandate to form a government on Nov. 8 after 67 lawmakers backed him. Netanyahu lost his majority, and the new Knesset was sworn in Tuesday without a coalition.
  • Likud is still the largest party with 27 seats, and Netanyahu's bloc has 53. Eisenkot's Yashar has 21 and his four-party alliance 48. Arab parties hold 19. Eisenkot has 28 days plus a possible 14-day extension.
  • His options, per Al-Monitor: a minority government backed by Arab parties, a deal with ultra-Orthodox parties, or a unity government with Netanyahu. Avigdor Lieberman rejects two of the three.

Left view

Al Jazeera and Al-Monitor stress how fragile any anti-Netanyahu coalition is and focus on whether Arab parties will finally get a role. The Times of Israel highlights friction: Eisenkot accused Netanyahu of refusing him a security briefing, which he is now set to receive.

Right view

JNS ran Netanyahu's argument that the "nation clearly decided" in Likud's favor as the largest party. Netanyahu is calling for a unity government of all Zionist parties and rules out relying on Arab parties.

Watch for

If Eisenkot fails, Netanyahu likely gets a turn, and a second failure could mean another election. The outcome affects Israel's posture on Gaza and Lebanon, and Lebanon is the first of Iran's seven conditions for ending the war.
