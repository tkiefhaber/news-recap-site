US and Iran explore phased deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz

Facts

  • Negotiators from both sides, meeting on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York, are discussing a staged approach: Iran reopens Hormuz and the U.S. lifts its economic blockade and possibly unfreezes assets in a first phase, per sources cited by Reuters.
  • A senior Iranian official said: “One way forward would be to solve the crisis in stages. The first would be to end the blockade and reopen Hormuz.” Iran is reportedly willing to defer its separate demand for Hormuz transit fees to get there.
  • Neither side wants to concede leverage first, and Trump has again suggested a full deal may not land until after the Nov. 3 midterms — a pattern of “imminent” predictions stretching back to early August that has yet to produce a signed agreement.
  • Former U.S. negotiator Dennis Ross put the odds of a pre-midterm deal at just 30%, per Reuters.

Left view

CNBC's coverage highlighted Iran's defiant public posture, reporting Tehran has called Trump's threats a sign of “strategic desperation” and is floating its own conditions rather than simply capitulating.

Right view

Fox News's rolling live coverage has repeatedly amplified administration claims that a Hormuz deal is close and that, as one White House official put it, Iran is “desperate” while Trump “holds all the cards.”

Watch for

Whether a first-phase deal actually lands to ease shipping and oil markets, or whether — as in prior rounds — both sides run out the clock to the midterms. A reopening would be the clearest near-term path to lower gas prices.

Trump and Xi wrap Washington summit; tariff truce extended, Taiwan and Iran unresolved

Facts

Right view

Fox News has covered Senate Republicans pitching health savings accounts as their preferred fix; Sen. Marsha Blackburn (R-Tenn.) called Democrats' shutdown-linked demands “ridiculous,” arguing Obamacare's own cost spiral undercuts the Democrats' narrative.

Watch for

Whether the House takes up the three-year extension in January despite the Senate's earlier rejection of similar bills. Open enrollment is underway now under the higher prices, raising health care's salience for midterm voters.
