The Big Stories Today

DOJ charges Comey a third time, over a 2016 New York Times story

Facts

  • Prosecutors unsealed a two-count criminal information Monday accusing former FBI Director James Comey of transmitting, and conspiring to transmit, national defense information tied to an Oct. 5, 2016 New York Times article (Axios, Time).
  • The information was filed under seal Oct. 5. It skips a grand jury; the DOJ says it was filed to stop the statute of limitations from expiring while the investigation continues. Judge Trevor McFadden unsealed it but has not ruled on a motion to dismiss.
  • Two earlier cases: the false-statements indictment was dismissed in November 2025 over the prosecutor's appointment, and a case over the “86 47” post is set for trial in February 2027.
  • Separately Monday, a federal judge ruled that Comey's daughter Maurene, a former prosecutor, was unlawfully fired (Fox News).

Left view

Time and Axios stress that two prosecutor teams in Trump's first term declined to charge this same episode, that the filing appears to break the unofficial 60-day pre-election norm, and that Comey calls it persecution. The Times says such cases aim to chill sources.

Right view

Fox News presents it as the third matter against Comey and relays the DOJ position that filing an information to toll the statute of limitations is routine. Its coverage cited no Republican lawmakers; mainstream and right-leaning coverage of the legal merits was otherwise thin this morning.

Watch for

Whether McFadden accepts an information instead of an indictment; the limitations-period fight; whether prosecutors seek a grand jury before the Nov. 3 election. Legal experts quoted in coverage see the timing as the main vulnerability.

US forces reportedly ready to hit Iran within 96 hours of an order; decision expected after the midterms

Facts

  • CNN, citing two US officials, reports forces can launch a major operation within 96 hours of a presidential order; targets could include nuclear, military and energy sites. Trump has not decided, and officials say any order is more likely after Nov. 3.
  • Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani officials met Iranian leaders in Tehran, with Qatari mediation, to head off escalation in the eight-month war. Iran says it will reopen Hormuz if the US lifts its blockade of Iranian ports; the US wants limits on enrichment.
  • Netanyahu reportedly asked for a joint strike before Israel's Oct. 27 election and Trump declined; his office denies this. The Houthis have attacked Saudi territory.
  • The US Strategic Petroleum Reserve is at its lowest level since 1982.

Left view

Coverage in outlets such as NPR focuses on the human and economic cost: jet fuel shortages, flight cuts, a nearly closed Hormuz, and concern that the timing is shaped by domestic politics.

Right view

Right-leaning framing, as in Fox News's Iran coverage, centers on Iranian attacks on shipping and mine-laying and on the blockade as leverage; Tehran's calls for a “single force” among Islamic nations get attention. I did not find a fresh right-leaning editorial on the strike timing today.

Watch for

Officials say Trump has appeared close to authorizing strikes before and backed off. Expect oil to react sharply to any order: Brent is about $115 and WTI about $101. Analysts cited in coverage warn that Hormuz risk keeps the fuel market tight into 2027.

Watch for
