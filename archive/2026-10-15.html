Bipartisan backlash grows over Trump's Russian diesel deal

Facts

  • Trump announced a deal with Putin on Oct. 9 to import Russian diesel to ease pump prices before the midterms. Diesel averaged $6.28 a gallon on Oct. 11, up 71% from a year earlier (Axios).
  • Six senators from both parties asked Trump to rescind it (NPR, Fortune). Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick has vowed to force a House vote to block the purchases; Sens. Murkowski, Collins, Tillis and Curtis have criticized it (Axios).
  • Critics say it conflicts with a 2022 import ban and with a new Russia sanctions law, which takes effect Oct. 18 (Fortune). Backers include Sen. John Barrasso and Rep. Anna Paulina Luna (Axios).
  • Trump says an energy ceasefire is in place; Zelenskyy says Russia kept striking Ukrainian energy sites (Fortune).

Left view

The Atlantic calls it a boost to Russia's faltering economy that undercuts sanctions; the Guardian cites analysts who say the volumes are too small to matter; the New York Times reports European governments are stunned.

Right view

Criticism is not confined to the left. National Review says the deal makes Trump look desperate, and the Washington Examiner calls it worse-than-useless economically. Defenders (Barrasso, Luna, the White House) frame it as a cost-of-living move. Right-leaning outlets that fully defend it were thin in today's search.

Watch for

Energy analysts quoted by Fortune and Axios doubt Russian diesel will meaningfully lower U.S. prices, since the spike stems mostly from the Iran war. Watch for a House vote, whether the Oct. 18 sanctions-law tariffs take effect, and European reaction. Axios notes the issue leaves GOP candidates with a Putin problem three weeks before Election Day.
